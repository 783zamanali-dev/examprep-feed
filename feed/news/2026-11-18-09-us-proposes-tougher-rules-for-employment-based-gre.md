WASHINGTON: The United States Department of Labour has published sweeping proposed changes to the rules governing employment-based green cards, requiring companies to make greater efforts to recruit American workers before sponsoring foreign employees for permanent residency.

The proposed overhaul would extend recruitment periods, restrict sponsorship by companies that have recently laid off US workers and eliminate special exemptions currently available to certain healthcare professionals.

Labour Secretary Keith Sonderling said existing rules had allowed companies to sponsor foreign workers “without fully considering American applicants”, a practice the department seeks to address through “expanded recruitment and enforcement requirements”.

The proposals would affect employers seeking to sponsor foreign nationals through the EB-2 and EB-3 employment-based green card categories under the Permanent Labour Certification (PERM) programme.

Under the existing system , employers must demonstrate that qualified American workers are unavailable for a particular position and that hiring a foreign worker would not adversely affect domestic wages or working conditions.

The proposed rules would increase the minimum recruitment period from 30 to 90 calendar days. Employers that normally advertise comparable vacancies for longer periods would be required to follow their established recruitment schedules.

Companies would also have to expand their use of digital recruitment platforms, including online job boards, corporate websites and social media. Job advertisements would be required to disclose offered wages or salary ranges, while employers would have to demonstrate that American applicants received fair consideration.

Employers would face stricter documentation requirements as well. They would have to submit recruitment reports, supporting records and the resumes of American applicants along with their labour certification applications. Under current regulations, much of this material is generally retained by employers and produced if an application is selected for a federal audit.

Another significant provision would restrict companies that have laid off American workers in the same or similar occupations within the same geographical area during the preceding two years from obtaining labour certification for those positions.

Existing regulations generally require employers to notify and consider potentially qualified American workers laid off within the previous six months. The proposed changes would extend the period during which such layoffs could affect sponsorship decisions.

The restrictions would also cover certain layoffs involving third-party staffing companies. Employers would have to report subsequent workforce reductions and continue considering qualified displaced American workers during the related immigration petition process.

Healthcare employers could face additional difficulties under the proposed overhaul.

The department plans to eliminate Schedule A, a provision that currently allows employers to sponsor professional nurses, physical therapists and certain individuals with exceptional abilities in the sciences or arts without completing the standard labour market recruitment process.

Removing the exemption would require hospitals and other healthcare facilities to advertise vacancies, consider qualified American applicants and obtain labour certification before proceeding with immigration petitions for foreign professionals.

The change could particularly affect healthcare institutions that rely on internationally recruited nurses and physical therapists.

The proposals would constitute the first comprehensive overhaul of PERM regulations in nearly two decades.

## Key facts

- ★ The proposed rules affect EB-2 and EB-3 green card categories under the PERM programme.
- ★ The minimum mandatory recruitment period for employers will increase from 30 to 90 days.
- ★ Employers are now required to disclose salary ranges in job advertisements.
- ★ Companies that have recently laid off American workers will face restricted ability to sponsor foreign employees.
