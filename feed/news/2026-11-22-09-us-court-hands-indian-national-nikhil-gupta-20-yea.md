WASHINGTON: An Indian national has been sentenced to 235 months in prison for his role in a plot allegedly directed by an Indian government official to assassinate a US-based Sikh nationalist leader on American soil, the US Department of Justice (DOJ) announced on Friday.

Nikhil Gupta, 55, also known as Nick, was sentenced by US District Judge Victor Marrero in the Southern District of New York after pleading guilty in February 2026 to murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit money laundering.

The case involved an alleged conspiracy to kill Gurpatwant Singh Pannun , a US and Canadian citizen, attorney and political activist who advocates the creation of an independent Sikh homeland, Khalistan, in India.

According to the DOJ, Gupta worked with Vikash Yadav, a co-defendant and Indian government employee, to organise the 2023 assassination. Yadav was employed by India’s cabinet secretariat that houses the country’s foreign intelligence service, the Research and Analysis Wing (RAW).

Additional details emerging from the court proceedings indicate that Gupta arranged a $15,000 advance payment to a person he believed was a hired assassin. The individual was actually an undercover US law enforcement officer.

Gupta and Yadav had discussed paying $100,000 for the assassination. Gupta also supplied information about Pannun to the supposed hitman to facilitate the killing.

Prosecutors said Yadav provided personal data of Pannun, including his home address, phone numbers and details of his daily movements. Gupta allegedly passed this information to the undercover operative and kept Yadav informed about the progress of the operation.

The plot was foiled by US agencies before Pannun could be killed.

Prosecution sought 24 years

Prosecutors had sought 293 months, citing his alleged role in a foreign-directed plot to assassinate Pannun, while the defence requested 144 months, citing his lack of a criminal record, alcohol dependence and personal circumstances.

During the hearing, Gupta apologised to Pannun, affected communities and his family, expressing regret for abandoning Mahatma Gandhi’s principle of non-violence.

Pannun thanked US authorities for prosecuting the case and reaffirmed his commitment to Sikh independence.

The case has also been linked to the June 2023 killing of Sikh nationalist leader Hardeep Singh Nijjar in Canada, though Gupta’s defence denied his involvement in Nijjar’s assassination.

Prosecutors had identified Indian official Yadav as the alleged director of the operation. He remains in India despite a US arrest warrant issued in October 2024, as India denies role in assassination plots.
