**Three tests Pakistan's social protection system must pass** (The Express Tribune · Dr Asghar Zaidi)

Writing from an ESCAP meeting on ageing in Bangkok, the writer proposes three tests for Pakistan's social protection system: fiscal responsibility, social adequacy and intergenerational fairness. He says Pakistan spends heavily on pensions for a few government employees while most older people get nothing. He suggests a universal tax-funded basic pension, a Senior Citizens Act and EOBI reform.

- ★ The Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing was signed in 2002 by 159 governments, including Pakistan.
- ★ Pakistan's federal pension bill this year is Rs1.055 trillion and has been growing by 20 to 30pc a year.
- ★ Since July 2024, new federal employees pay 10pc of salary into a pension fund and the state adds 12pc.
- ★ EOBI pays old-age pensions to about 323,000 people, with a minimum pension of Rs11,500 a month.
- ★ HelpAge International estimates only 3 million of Pakistan's 15 million older people receive any pension.
- ★ Asia and the Pacific has 748 million people aged 60 and over, expected to reach 1.33 billion by 2050.

**Lahore’s PM2.5 level eight times WHO guideline, report says** (Dawn · Imran Gabol)

A Pakistan Air Quality Initiative report found 16,714 likely crop-residue fires in Punjab between October 1 and November 9. Lahore's average PM2.5 level was 126 micrograms per cubic metre, eight times the WHO guideline. The report said evidence did not support blaming Lahore's post-Diwali pollution on Indian fireworks, as inland cities saw bigger rises. Punjab's environment department reported a sharp AQI improvement after anti-smog operations.

- ★ PAQI detected 16,714 likely crop-residue fires in Punjab between Oct 1 and Nov 9, 2026.
- ★ Lahore's average PM2.5 concentration was 126 micrograms per cubic metre, eight times the WHO guideline and 11pc lower than in 2025.
- ★ Sheikhupura recorded the most fires (2,559), followed by Jhang, Okara, Hafizabad and Gujranwala.
- ★ The report found the evidence did not support blaming Lahore's post-Diwali pollution on Indian fireworks.
- ★ Lahore's average AQI fell from 489 to 232 on one day, with improvement at all 11 monitoring stations.
