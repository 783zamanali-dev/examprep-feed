KHYBER: With the return of nearly 2,000 selected displaced persons to the restive Tirah Valley since last week, a number of shops in the main market have reopened after 11 months of closure.

Sources among the returned persons told Dawn that shops mostly dealing in edibles, medicines and construction tools were reopened with the return of at least 60 shopkeepers of Lar Bagh Markaz in Maidan area, the main trading centre of Tirah Valley.

They said the main Jamia Masjid of Bagh Markaz was also reopened with a handful of the returned people offering their first Friday prayers in the mosque after 11 months.

They said that a butcher had also slaughtered an animal after a long break with a number of the returned persons buying fresh beef from his outlet. A restaurant owner also arranged for the valley’s famous “Karrechi” (beef karahi) for interested buyers.

Sources said that though the transport fare for commercial goods up to Tirah Valley had increased almost three times from Rs6,000 to Rs18,000 one way, people who had recently returned were in dire need of daily use items. They said that local shopkeepers arranged for supply of those items, albeit with payment of additional charges to transporters.

They said that a number of the returned persons also started repairing the damaged portions of their houses without any financial assistance from the government.

The district administration had earlier announced that the returning people would not be entitled to any monetary assistance with all of them required to arrange their transportation, food and repair or reconstruction of their personal properties at their own expense.

Suhbat Khan Afridi, a spokesman for the displaced Tirah residents, told this scribe that though shops at Lar Bagh Markaz had reopened on a limited scale, reconstruction or repair of the damaged houses was a difficult exercise for them.

He said that most of the displaced families could not afford the reconstruction expenses while harsh cold weather was also a serious impediment in the reconstruction activities.

He also complained that the district administration delayed displaying the list of the approved returnees, leaving many to find out a day or two late. He suggested that names of the selected persons should be displayed at prominent places at least two days in advance of their return.
