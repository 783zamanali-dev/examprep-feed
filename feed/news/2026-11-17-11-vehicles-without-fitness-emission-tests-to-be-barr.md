ISLAMABAD: The government on Tuesday decided to bar vehicles without fitness and emission testing from entering Islamabad, as part of measures to curb smog and air pollution in the capital.

The decision was taken at a meeting chaired by Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry to review steps for smog prevention and reduction of air pollution.

The meeting reviewed the current smog situation in Islamabad, the Air Quality Index (AQI) and ongoing measures to control air pollution.

Chaudhry directed all relevant departments to ensure coordinated and effective measures to control air pollution and prevent smog. He said Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi was himself monitoring the entire process of anti-smog measures in Islamabad and their implementation.

He contended that Islamabad’s AQI in October and November this year had remained better than the same period last year.

According to data published by the Pakistan Environmental Protection Agency (Pak-EPA), the average AQI index for Islamabad was 148 this October , compared to an average of 169 in October 2025 .

During the meeting, it was decided that heavy fines would be imposed on those involved in open burning and other smog-causing activities.

Chaudhry warned of first information reports, arrests and heavy fines against violators of environmental laws. Strict action would also be taken against smoke-emitting brick kilns and industrial units, he added.

The minister stressed that public cooperation was crucial for smog control and improvement in air quality, and citizens would have to play a responsible role.

The additional deputy commissioner (ADC) (General), ADC (Revenue), the senior superintendent of police (Operations) and the chief traffic officer attended the meeting, alongside the Pak-EPA and excise directors and other relevant officials.

In the past few years, air pollution has become one of the most pressing public health and environmental crises facing Pakistan. Various cities deal with smog, attributed mainly to industrial emissions, vehicular pollution , stubble burning and air movement .

Last month, a two-day Pakistan National Clean Air Conference concluded with the adoption of the Islamabad Clean Air Compact, calling for coordinated federal and provincial action, stricter enforcement, regular emissions inventories and annual reviews to tackle air pollution.

The National Assembly Standing Committee on Climate Change and Environmental Coordination has recommended the introduction of a regular vehicle emission testing mechanism in Islamabad and called for updating the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act, 1997, to bring it in line with current environmental challenges and international commitments.

Last year, Pak-EPA launched large-scale inspections of vehicles and industrial units, while demolishing non-compliant brick kilns as part of a multi-tier plan to curb escalating air pollution.

## Key facts

- ★ The ban targets vehicles lacking valid fitness and emission testing.
- ★ The average Air Quality Index (AQI) for Islamabad was 148 in October 2026.

- ★ Violators face potential First Information Reports (FIRs) and heavy fines.
- ★ The policy targets smoke-emitting industrial units and brick kilns alongside road traffic.

## Vocabulary

- **Smog** — Fog or haze intensified by smoke and other atmospheric pollutants.
- **Emissions** — The production and discharge of something, especially gas or radiation.
