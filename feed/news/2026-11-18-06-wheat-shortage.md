> Why it matters: It highlights systemic failures in agricultural policy and food security management, which are frequent themes in exam questions.



Rising wheat and flour prices have placed another burden on Pakistani households, exposing serious shortcomings in the country's management of its most essential food commodity. Despite the arrival of more than 100,000 tonnes of imported wheat since late October, flour prices continue to climb, with some varieties now selling for as much as Rs200 per kg. Market estimates suggest a shortfall of three to four million tonnes before the next harvest, which in turn raises serious questions about the government's ability to anticipate demand and maintain adequate supplies. Pakistan's recurring wheat shortages cannot be attributed to inadequate production alone. The country harvested 29.61 million tonnes in FY26, an increase of 4.3% over the previous year, yet production remains insufficient to meet annual consumption of 31 to 32 million tonnes. With population growth steadily increasing demand, the absence of reliable forecasting, effective stock management and timely import decisions has compounded the problem. Warnings of an impending shortage were reportedly issued as early as May, but the authorities failed to respond with the urgency required. Equally concerning is the apparent disconnect between official assurances of sufficient wheat stocks and the realities of the market. Suspected hoarding and disruptions in interprovincial supplies have contributed to price volatility, while the benefits of imported wheat have yet to reach consumers. Dependence on imports to bridge domestic shortages also places an unnecessary strain on foreign exchange reserves. Federal and provincial authorities must therefore urgently reconcile production and consumption to ensure transparent distribution until the next harvest. Stronger market oversight and coordinated procurement arrangements are equally necessary. Ensuring an affordable and uninterrupted wheat supply - that too at reasonable prices - must remain a central priority of the country's agricultural and economic policies.

## Key facts

- ★ Pakistan produced 29.61 million tonnes of wheat in FY26, a 4.3% increase from the previous year.
- ★ The annual national consumption of wheat is estimated between 31 and 32 million tonnes.
- ★ Market estimates suggest a supply shortfall of three to four million tonnes before the next harvest.
- ★ Over 100,000 tonnes of wheat have been imported since late October.
- ★ Flour prices in some markets have reached Rs200 per kg.
