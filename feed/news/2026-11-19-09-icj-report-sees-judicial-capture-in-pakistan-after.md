> Why it matters: It provides an international legal perspective on significant constitutional changes that impact the separation of powers and judicial independence in Pakistan.



A report released by the International Commission of Jurists (ICJ) on Wednesday said that the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments led to a “systemic judicial capture” in Pakistan.

The 26th Amendment , passed in October 2024, carved constitutional benches out of the Supreme Court, with their members chosen by a reconstituted Judicial Commission of Pakistan (JCP) in which judges no longer held a majority.

Barely a year later, the 27th Amendment turned those benches into an entirely separate court, the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC). It also made significant changes to the judicial system, the military’s chain of command and granted immunities to government officials. Two of the SC’s senior-most judges resigned in protest the day it became law.

On Wednesday, the ICJ released a report — titled ‘ Judicial Capture: Independence of the Judiciary in Pakistan after the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments ’.

It said that after analysing the institutional framework governing higher courts following the two amendments, the judiciary in Pakistan no longer enjoyed institutional guarantees of independence required under international law and standards.

“The 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments were a decisive break from the model of judicial independence that Pakistan had built after the restoration of the judiciary in 2009,” the report noted.

However, it observed that the “erosion of judicial independence” was done through constitutional amendments, rather than the overt removal of judges.

The report said that while the government argued that amendments were made to “check judicial overreach, reduce the backlog of cases, end the perceived politicisation of the SC’s original jurisdiction and give Parliament a role in judicial appointments comparable to that of legislatures in other democracies”, the claims did not justify “changing the judicial structure in a manner that is glaringly inconsistent with Pakistan’s obligations under international law”.

It also acknowledged that the Supreme Court had “encroached into roles of Parliament and other institutions”. However, it also noted that with the 26th and 27th amendments, the Parliament failed to ensure these “deficiencies” were fixed in line with judicial independence.

“Instead, the 26th and 27th Amendments have led to systemic judicial capture by other branches of the state and the dismantling of the rule of law in the country,” the ICJ stated.

It was found that the ruling coalition had “exploited” the public’s grievances over the functioning of courts in a “self-serving manner” to control the judiciary.

“As a result, even the highest courts of the land in Pakistan no longer serve as a check on the executive or the Parliament and their function as avenues for redress of victims of human rights violations is seriously impaired,” the report stated.

Call for comprehensive review

In its recommendations, the report appealed to the government to begin a comprehensive review of the amendments and their implementation in “light of international law and standards”, particularly under the framework of Pakistan’s obligations under the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) and by holding “transparent and broad” public consultation.

It suggested that the government engage in international cooperation, including seeking technical assistance from the UN special rapporteur on the independence of judges and lawyers and inviting them for a country visit.

In its recommendations to the JCP, the ICJ called on it to revise the criteria for judicial appointments in accordance with international law, standards and best practices.

It further suggested: “Ensure the published criteria are the sole basis for nomination of judges to the FCC, the SC, the high courts and constitutional benches of the high courts.”

It also called on the commission to establish a transparent procedure for appointments and publish reasons for each appointment that “demonstrate the published criteria are being implemented in practice”.

It also called on the JCP to refrain from transferring a high court judge without their consent.

In its recommendations to the Supreme Judicial Council, the ICJ said: “Define ‘inefficiency’ in a manner that is consistent with international standards to only mean serious incompetence that renders judges unfit to discharge their duties.”

For the Special Parliamentary Committee , which has the power to nominate the chief justice of Pakistan, the report called for it to adopt “clear and transparent criteria and procedures” for the appointment of the SC and FCC top judges and to publish the reasons for each selection.

Lastly, the report urged the FCC, the SC and the high courts to “interpret and apply the provisions of the 26th and 27th Amendments, to the greatest extent possible, in a manner consistent with the independence of the judiciary as guaranteed by Article 14 of the ICCPR”.

## Key facts

- ★ The 26th Amendment was passed in October 2024.
- ★ The 27th Amendment established the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC).
- ★ The ICJ report is titled 'Judicial Capture: Independence of the Judiciary in Pakistan after the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments'.
- ★ Two Supreme Court judges resigned in protest following the 27th Amendment.

## Vocabulary

- **Judicial capture** — A form of political corruption where the judiciary is manipulated by other branches of government.
- **Overt** — Done or shown openly; not secret.
