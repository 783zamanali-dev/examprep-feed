> Why it matters: It highlights critical issues in public resource management, provincial inequality, and the failure of governance regarding basic social indicators.



The Sindh Assembly's decision to set aside nearly Rs5 billion for its own expenses at a time when more than half of the province's population lives in multidimensional poverty may be the most telling illustration of the misplaced priorities of the ruling class. At a time when families across Sindh struggle to afford two meals a day and about one-third of the province is below the official poverty line, the provincial government's planning priorities are nothing less than immoral. The Assembly Secretariat alone has been allocated Rs2.84 billion, including Rs1.31 billion in non-salary expenses. Legislators receive Rs1.476 billion, of which Rs592 million is for salaries, and Rs371 million for travel and daily allowances, and Rs132 million for other perks. Ministers and special assistants enjoy another Rs766 million, plus Rs231 million for protocol. Meanwhile, Rs217 million was spent under a single "Others" head with no breakdown, and Rs128 million went to "Entertainment" over four years - all while the assembly's accounts escape meaningful audit scrutiny. This extravagant spending does not occur in a vacuum. The UNDP's Global Multidimensional Poverty Index says over half of Sindh's population is in deprivation across healthcare, education and nutrition, with 71% of the poor concentrated in rural Sindh. Sindh also has the country's highest inequality rate of 35.9%, with feudalism in most of the province and the extreme concentration of wealth in Karachi being the main contributing factors. While the government may contort its way into creating excuses, the fact is that development spending - which benefits the neediest people - has been slashed while spending on MPAs' lifestyle development continues to rise. The message to ordinary citizens is clear - their children's empty bellies are an afterthought, and thanks to de facto one-party rule in the province, there is little incentive for rulers to mend their ways.

## Key facts

- ★ The Sindh Assembly allocated approximately Rs5 billion for its own annual expenses.
- ★ Over 50 percent of the population in Sindh lives in multidimensional poverty.
- ★ Sindh holds the highest inequality rate in Pakistan at 35.9 percent.
- ★ According to the UNDP, 71 percent of the poor in Sindh are concentrated in rural areas.

## Vocabulary

- **multidimensional** — Involving several distinct aspects or factors.
- **deprivation** — The damaging lack of basic material benefits.
