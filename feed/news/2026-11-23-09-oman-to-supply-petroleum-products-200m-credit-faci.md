> Why it matters: It illustrates Pakistan's strategic efforts to maintain energy security amidst geopolitical tensions and economic constraints.



ISLAMABAD: Pakistan and Oman on Sunday signed an agreement under which the Gulf country’s state-owned OQ Trading would supply petroleum products, as well as a $200 million credit line, to the Pakistan State Oil (PSO).

This could provide Pakistan with a means to strategically diversify its sourcing of petroleum products as the Strait of Hormuz continues to be choked by the US-Iran conflict into its ninth month.

According to an announcement from the Petroleum Division, the agreement between the two state-owned entities was signed during Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik’s visit to Muscat, where he met with Oman’s Minister of Energy and Minerals Eng. Salim Al Aufi.

The two sides discussed opportunities for strengthening bilateral cooperation in the oil, gas and minerals sectors.

During the meeting, both ministers discussed avenues for mutually beneficial cooperation and welcomed the establishment of the Pakistan-Oman Joint Working Group on Oil and Gas. They agreed to make optimal use of this institutional mechanism to further strengthen bilateral engagement and advance cooperation in the energy and mining sector.

Following the meeting, the two ministers witnessed the signing of a Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA) for petroleum products between PSO and OQ Trading (OQT), Oman’s global commodity trading company. OQT has also agreed to extend a $200m credit line to PSO to facilitate petroleum purchases.

Welcoming the agreement, both ministers expressed confidence that its implementation would further strengthen Pakistan-Oman energy ties and support Pakistan’s efforts to enhance energy security through diversification of energy imports.

“The agreement marks an important milestone in the commercial relationship between the two companies and provides a structured arrangement for petroleum product supplies from OQT to PSO, supporting supply reliability amid continued volatility in regional energy markets,” the Petroleum Division’s statement said.

It noted that OQT has already established itself as a major supplier of gasoline to PSO, supplying more than 1m tonnes during 2026. The newly signed agreement builds on this established trading relationship and provides a framework for expanding commercial cooperation, the statement added.

Beyond gasoline, OQT has also strengthened its presence in Pakistan’s liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) market, supplying both state-owned and private sector buyers.

According to the Petroleum Division, OQT’s LPG supplies to Pakistan are expected to exceed 120,000 metric tonnes by the end of 2026, reflecting the company’s continued commitment to meeting market demand and maintaining energy flows amid regional supply disruptions.

The meeting and the signing of the agreement underscore the shared commitment of Pakistan and Oman to expanding bilateral energy cooperation, strengthening commercial partnerships and exploring new investment opportunities across the oil, gas and minerals sectors, the statement added.

Last month, Oman’s Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi made a two-day visit to Pakistan, meeting its top leadership.

He attended the eighth session of the Pakistan-Oman Joint Ministerial Commission (JMC), during which both sides emphasised the importance of promoting exchanges of business delegations from both countries to unlock new opportunities for trade, investment and institutional collaboration.

## Key facts

- ★ Pakistan and Oman signed a Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA) for petroleum products.
- ★ The agreement provides a $200 million credit line to Pakistan State Oil (PSO).
- ★ The deal aims to diversify energy sources as the Strait of Hormuz faces instability.
- ★ A Pakistan-Oman Joint Working Group on Oil and Gas has been established to strengthen bilateral cooperation.

## Vocabulary

- **Diversify** — To broaden the range of sources or activities.
