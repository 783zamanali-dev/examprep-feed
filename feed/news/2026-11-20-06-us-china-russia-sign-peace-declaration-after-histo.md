> Why it matters: A historic trilateral summit between major global powers with direct implications for global strategic stability and UN reform.



China, the United States and Russia issued a “Declaration toward Peace” on Thursday after their leaders held an unprecedented summit, laying out common ambitions to overhaul the UN and keep the International Criminal Court (ICC) in check.

The statement, issued after Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin met in China, said they wanted to protect diplomatic immunity for state officials and also boost supply and production of refined petroleum products.

“The three heads of state had an in-depth exchange of views on the current international situation and committed to maintaining strategic stability and international security,” a White House version of the statement said.

“The three heads of state reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. The three countries will conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons.”

Beijing has long maintained that the United States and Russia must reduce their giant nuclear arsenals before it agrees to talks on its smaller stockpile.

The huddle took place with Russia’s years-long offensive in Ukraine grinding on, Trump bogged down in the Middle East , and an ascendant China looking to strengthen its hand in East Asia.

“We discussed the question of democracy. And what is democracy? It’s what? It’s the liberal order that certain circles put together and still now are trying to impose on the whole world,” Putin told a news conference in Shenzhen.

The three men made opening remarks touching on weighty topics from nuclear weapons to the war in Ukraine as they met around a large, dark round table with a pink flower arrangement at its centre.

The discussion behind closed doors in Kylin Villa ended after an hour and 20 minutes.

“We are really making history,” Trump told Xi and Putin at the start of the meeting before journalists were sent away.

“No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement.”

“The three countries share a common perspective on safeguarding world peace,” Xi said, while Putin said they wrote a new “page in the history of diplomacy”.

Trump had insisted nuclear arms control be a central topic of the summit.

“It’s not an easy thing to do, give them up,” Trump told Xi and Putin as the meeting began.

None of the presidents made immediate statements after their meeting, and Trump departed on his flight back to the US.

ICC, UN and oil

In their joint statement, the leaders “recognised that the principle of state consent and respect for immunities of State officials are of paramount importance, and concurred that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction”.

Putin is subject to an ICC arrest warrant, while Trump’s administration has vowed to dismantle the court “brick by brick”.

The leaders said they were “committed to reform the UN so that the UN can respond more effectively to global challenges and conform to its fundamental mission of international peace and security”.

The joint statement said the countries “plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations. They reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows”.

The summit stoked fears that the three powerful men want to divide the globe into spheres of influence, for example by making decisions impacting Ukraine over the heads of Kyiv or Europe.

Differences over Ukraine, Iran and trade

Earlier on Thursday, Trump and Putin held a meeting — their first since August 2025 — with the 80-year-old US president calling the Russian his “friend”.

Putin responded in English: “Nice to meet you.”

Trump and Xi then came together — their third time this year — for one-on-one discussions, with the US leader highlighting a “very special relationship”.

Putin, 74, and Xi, 73, held talks on Wednesday — their 50th meeting, the Chinese president said.

Trump and Xi met after an extension of their trade truce this month, but also amid growing tension over reports of Nvidia chips being diverted to China through Southeast Asia, raising questions about the effectiveness of US export curbs and the future of Chinese access to advanced technology.

The three countries have deep differences over Ukraine, Iran, nuclear weapons and trade. Their meeting, on the sidelines of an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit, has unnerved smaller countries that fear their interests might be disregarded.

Putin, fresh from a setback in his war against Ukraine after the crippling of a key bridge linking Crimea to Russia, said the three countries had “numerous shared interests in the settlement of regional conflicts, including obviously the conflict in Ukraine”.

Unlike Trump and Putin, Xi did not make a direct reference to Ukraine, but said: “History tells us that when major powers live in harmony, the world is at peace. When major powers clash, the world falls into turmoil.”

Trump said both he and Xi felt strongly about oil having to flow through the Strait of Hormuz, which has been disrupted by the Iran war.

Xi did not directly mention Iran in any of his remarks.

“China, Russia, and the United States should continue encouraging parties to conflicts to sit down and engage in talks, and resolve disputes through political and diplomatic means,” Xi said.

The Kylin Villa where they met has a long history of sensitive diplomatic encounters. Its past visitors include former North Korean leader Kim Jong-il, Xi’s father Xi Zhongxun, and envoys of the Dalai Lama.

Airport welcome

Breaking with decades of diplomatic protocol, Xi received Trump at the airport, with the men briefly hugging before a red-carpet greeting complete with cheering children and an air-force flyover.

This reciprocated the airport greeting Trump gave Xi at Joint Base Andrews in September.

“It’s a friendship relationship. It’s a respect relationship,” Trump told Fox News.

The airport welcome showed “great respect, and I appreciate that. That’s a big win for us. It’s a big win for China too,” Trump said.

Other world leaders at the Apec meeting included Japan’s Sanae Takaichi, South Korea’s Lee Jae Myung, Canada’s Mark Carney and Australia’s Anthony Albanese.

Takaichi, on her first visit since angering Beijing a year ago with comments about Taiwan, met Putin — relations are also tense — on Wednesday, Tokyo said.

Takaichi, who by Thursday afternoon had not held any formal meeting with Xi, said on Thursday she wanted a “constructive and stable relationship” with China.

Despite their differences, all the leaders — including Putin — posed for the Apec “family photo” on Thursday in special scarves.

Xi opened the Apec leaders’ meeting on Thursday with a call to “reduce barriers to trade and investment”.

## Key facts

- ★ US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin met for an 80-minute summit at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen.
- ★ The three leaders issued a trilateral document titled Declaration toward Peace.
- ★ The declaration reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.
- ★ The leaders agreed to work on overhauling the United Nations and keeping the International Criminal Court (ICC) in check.
- ★ Discussions also included boosting the production and supply of refined petroleum products.

## Vocabulary

- **proliferation** — rapid increase or spread, especially of nuclear weapons
- **arsenal** — a collection of weapons and military equipment
