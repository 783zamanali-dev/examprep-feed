The government of Prime Minister Narendra Modi continues to find new ways to insult the intelligence of its citizens and assault the rights of the country's minorities. The deletion of 130 million names from India's electoral rolls - the largest such purge in the nation's history - has nothing to do with electoral integrity, but rather is a bald-faced effort to disenfranchise Muslims and others who are unlikely to support the ruling BJP. In West Bengal, for example, Muslims constituted approximately 70% of the 2.7 million names deleted after closer review, despite making up only 27% of the population. Nationwide, rights groups have documented that the omissions fall disproportionately on religious minorities, Dalits and other marginalised communities. The consequences of removal from the rolls do not end with just disenfranchisement. In many cases, victims of the BJP's rigging exercise have wound up being detained, deported and stripped of citizenship. There have been cases of people being deported to countries they had never been to before. Home Minister Amit Shah - Modi's closest adviser - said out the quiet part loud at a recent rally in Haryana, explicitly linking voter roll revision to denaturalisation and deportation. If Shah's words reflect the actual thinking of the Indian government, the voter roll purges are not just a human rights violation, but could even meet the criteria for ethnic cleansing. It is also worth noting that the purges have already borne fruit. After deleting nine million voters, the BJP won elections in West Bengal for the first time ever. People protesting against the voter purge were beaten and jailed. Meanwhile, the prime minister has failed even to try to properly defend either the policy or its enforcement. But just as he has failed to hold a single actual press conference in almost 12 years as prime minister, Modi is getting what he wanted without having to answer to anyone, as India continues to move toward being a democracy for only the right kinds of people.

## Key facts

- ★ Approximately 130 million names have been removed from India's electoral rolls.
- ★ In West Bengal, Muslims made up 70% of the 2.7 million deleted names despite being 27% of the state's population.
- ★ Home Minister Amit Shah has linked voter roll revisions to issues of denaturalisation and deportation.
- ★ The BJP reportedly won elections in West Bengal for the first time after the deletion of nine million voters from the rolls.

## Vocabulary

- **Disenfranchise** — To deprive someone of the right to vote.
- **Purge** — To remove people or things from an organization in a sudden or violent way.
- **Denaturalisation** — The act of taking away citizenship from a naturalized citizen.
