> Why it matters: Crucial for essays and questions on global climate diplomacy, COP negotiations, and international climate finance.



Global climate negotiations have reached a point where even acknowledging the principal cause of global warming appears to be beyond the willingness of major economies. After more than three decades of international conferences, governments are still reluctant to commit themselves to reducing their dependence on fossil fuels, despite mounting scientific evidence and increasingly destructive climate disasters. COP31 in Antalya, T&uuml;rkiye, was expected to advance the transition agreed upon in Dubai in 2023. Instead, it has exposed how readily political and commercial interests can undermine climate commitments. A revised draft agreement has removed all references to transitioning away from coal, oil and gas, while placing considerable emphasis on carbon capture and storage technologies. Although these technologies may have a role in reducing emissions, they cannot substitute for a systematic reduction in fossil fuel consumption. Their adoption on the scale envisaged remains uncertain, and presenting them as a principal solution offers energy producers an opportunity to prolong existing practices. While an agreement may emerge from the negotiations, its value will be severely diminished if it fails to address the fossil fuel transition. Britain's prime minister was the only G7 head of government to attend the summit, reflecting the weakening leadership of First World countries. Their reluctance to commit to the transition has wider consequences, particularly when developing countries are expected to pursue costly transitions. Worse still is the retreat on climate finance. The proposed annual adaptation funding target of $120 billion by 2035 has disappeared from the revised draft, leaving developing countries with another uncertain promise of future discussions. COP31 has already proven to be a failure - that is the bottom line. Unless governments commit to a credible fossil fuel transition, climate summits like this will continue to produce declarations that grow progressively more detached from the realities confronting our world.

## Key facts

- ★ COP31 took place in Antalya, Turkiye, following transitions negotiated in Dubai in 2023.
- ★ A revised draft agreement dropped all references to transitioning away from coal, oil, and gas.
- ★ The draft removed a proposed adaptation funding target of $120 billion per year by 2035.
- ★ Britain's prime minister was the only G7 head of government to attend the summit.
