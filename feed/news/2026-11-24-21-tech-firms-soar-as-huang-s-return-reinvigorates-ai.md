Technology firms rocketed Tuesday as Asia joined a global stock surge following the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia boss less than two weeks after stepping down over extramarital affairs including with an alleged Chinese spy .

The Silicon Valley giant’s resignation on November 12 sent shockwaves through markets and dealt a severe blow to the AI trade, which was already under pressure amid worries over profitability and the vast sums pumped into the sector.

His role as a fixer and visionary in an industry that has fuelled a tech revolution and a global economic boom was seen as crucial to the firm’s future as well as the AI buildout.

News of his return — announced soon after the opening bell Monday — lit a fire under equities, sending shares in Nvidia soaring an eye-watering 31.5 per cent and adding more than $900 billion to the firm’s market value.

Other Wall Street titans also enjoyed much-needed buying, pulling the Nasdaq up more than 6pc, the S&P 500 4.7pc higher and the Dow 3pc higher.

And Asia, which had started the week on the back foot, followed suit.

Taipei was the standout, blasting more than 9pc higher as chip titans TSMC and MediaTek added 10pc each, and Seoul piled on more than 6pc thanks to gains of around 8pc in market heavyweights SK hynix and Samsung.

Tokyo was lifted four percent by spikes of 14pc in Advantest, 11 percent in Tokyo Electron and 8pc in Kioxia.

Hong Kong, Shanghai, Sydney, Singapore, Wellington and Manila also joined in the fun with healthy advances.

“Jensen’s return takes away one of the biggest unknowns hanging over the AI trade,” said Saxo Markets’ Charu Chanana.

“He is not just Nvidia’s CEO; he is arguably the biggest salesman for the entire AI ecosystem. His relationships with customers, suppliers and capital providers are difficult to replicate.

“(His) return is particularly welcome for Asian technology stocks, many of which suffered collateral damage from Nvidia’s leadership crisis.”

And National Australia Bank’s Gavin Friend said: “The AI trade has been given a reprieve, with Huang’s reinstatement assuaging fears of any unravelling of the circular financing activity that has been a key support for the sector.”

However, analysts added that Huang’s reinstatement — which President Donald Trump said followed his own direct contacts with the company’s board — did not mean an end to probes or the uncertainty that has clouded the AI trade for the past six months.

The company is still under scrutiny from investigators including at the FBI and on Capitol Hill.

Huang reportedly had a relationship with Alice Huang — who is not related — a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed. The group is under investigation for allegedly helping Chinese firms gain access to Nvidia’s top-line chips.

Just this weekend, The New York Times added further details to the allegations, saying Alice Huang had been in touch with China’s top spy agency during the secret affair.

The United States on Monday added Megaspeed to a trade blacklist that bars it from purchasing advanced chips.

Oil prices continued to fall after Monday’s sharp drop that saw WTI back below $80 for the first time since August.

The latest selling was fuelled by hopes for an end to the Iran conflict as Axios reported US Vice President JD Vance met the prime minister of Qatar, which has acted as a mediator in efforts to resolve the near nine-month conflict.
