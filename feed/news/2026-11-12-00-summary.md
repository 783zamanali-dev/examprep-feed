**Poison in the air** (The Express Tribune · editorial)

The Express Tribune calls Pakistan's air pollution a public health emergency. Data from the Pakistan Air Quality Initiative show Punjab's anti-smog measures have not worked, and local crop burning, not Indian Diwali fireworks, appears to be the main driver. Lahore's average PM2.5 was eight times the WHO daily guideline. The paper calls for a year-round clean-air strategy with enforceable targets.

- ★ Satellites detected 16,714 likely crop-residue fires in Pakistan between October 1 and November 9, 2026.
- ★ Lahore's average PM2.5 concentration was 126 micrograms per cubic metre, eight times the WHO daily guideline.
- ★ PAQI found insufficient evidence to link the deterioration to Diwali fireworks in India.
- ★ The University of Chicago's Air Quality Life Index says an average Pakistani could gain 3.3 years of life expectancy if pollution met WHO guidelines.
- ★ The Punjab Clean Air Programme is supported by $300 million in World Bank financing.
