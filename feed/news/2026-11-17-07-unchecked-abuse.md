> Why it matters: It highlights critical failures in governance, child protection, and human rights implementation within the Pakistani context.



Abuse of domestic workers in Pakistan persists largely because those subjected to it have few means of seeking protection or justice. Poverty compels thousands of families to send their children into domestic employment, often without any assurance of their safety or wellbeing. Within private households, these children remain entirely dependent on their employers, with little opportunity to report mistreatment or escape abusive conditions. The absence of effective oversight has allowed exploitation to continue despite laws intended to protect vulnerable workers. The death of 13-year-old Fatima, who was employed at the Islamabad residence of a senior government officer, raises questions about the protection afforded to children working in private homes. Preliminary medical findings reportedly revealed marks of physical violence on her body, while allegations that local police initially attempted to suppress the matter require investigation. The circumstances of her death must be established and those responsible, if found guilty, held accountable irrespective of their official position or social standing. Domestic employment remains one of the least regulated forms of labour in Pakistan. Workers frequently lack access to complaint mechanisms. Children face additional risks because they are less able to resist exploitation or recognise violations of their rights. Their employment also deprives them of education and opportunities for social mobility, perpetuating the very poverty that forces them into work. Legislation alone cannot address these conditions. Although the Islamabad Capital Territory Domestic Workers Act, 2022, prohibits the employment of children below 16, enforcement remains inadequate. Provincial governments must ensure that existing labour protections are implemented and that violations are investigated without interference. Effective complaint mechanisms and stronger child protection services are essential to help protect this vulnerable workforce. We, as a society, must realise that domestic workers are entitled to the same dignity and legal protection as other workers.

## Key facts

- ★ The Islamabad Capital Territory Domestic Workers Act, 2022, prohibits the employment of children below 16 years of age.
- ★ Domestic employment is currently one of the least regulated labour sectors in Pakistan.
- ★ Systemic poverty forces many families to send children into domestic service, hindering their education and social mobility.
- ★ Accountability for domestic abuse often fails because workers lack accessible complaint mechanisms.
