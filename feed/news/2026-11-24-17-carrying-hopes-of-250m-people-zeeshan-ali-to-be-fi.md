> Why it matters: This marks a significant national achievement in space exploration and highlights strengthening Pakistan-China cooperation in science and technology.



Zeeshan Ali was named a crew member of China’s Shenzhou-24 space mission on Tuesday, which is scheduled to launch on November 25, with Ali set to become the first Pakistani to undertake an orbital space mission .

Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal, who is in China, referred to Ali on X as “Pakistan’s first astronaut”.

Ali will also be the first foreign national to board Beijing’s Tiangong space station, the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) announced.

“Pakistani astronaut Mohammad Zeeshan Ali is the first foreign national to visit Tiangong,” CMSA Spokesperson Zhang Jingbo said during a press conference.

And the Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson said the Pakistani astronaut was also the first international crew member to join China’s manned space programme.

Jingbo said that the Long March-2F rocket that will carry the crew into space will soon be filled with propellant. “The crew is in good condition and the space station is functioning normally, meeting all requirements for the mission,” said Zhang.

According to the CMSA, the Pakistani astronaut will serve as a payload specialist during the mission and return aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, which docked with the Tiangong space station in May.

China’s astronaut Tang Hongbo will command the mission, with Wu Yi serving as the mission pilot and Ali taking on the role of payload specialist, according to the CMSA.

The crewed spaceship is scheduled to be launched at 8:18am (Beijing Time and 5:18am Pakistan Time) on Wednesday from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China.

Ali is expected to spend around a week in space, conducting various experiments alongside the Chinese crew. CMSA said that during the mission, the crew will conduct around 90 experiments.

The Chinese space agency said the mission’s objectives included conducting an orbital rotation with the crew of the earlier Shenzhou-23 mission, carrying out routine operations and maintenance of the Chinese space station, and continuing space science research, including experiments involving space payloads.

The crew currently aboard the Tiangong space station is headed by 40-year-old Zhu Yangzhu, Lai Ka-ying and Zhang Zhiyuan, also 40.

Lai is Hong Kong’s first astronaut and the only woman currently aboard the space station.

After the new crew reaches the space station, Ali will return to Earth alongside Lai and Zhang.

Zhu will remain on board to complete China’s first full-year stay in space — a key test of endurance critical for future lunar and potentially deep-space ambitions.

Zhu will be accompanied for the next six-month Tiangong rotation by 51-year-old space veteran Hongbo and Yi, in his late thirties and making his first voyage to space.

‘We have already made history’

Speaking at a press briefing in Jiayuguan, Ali said he was “deeply grateful” to the Chinese government for helping “us realise our dream of going into space”.

He said he arrived at the Astronaut Centre of China a few months ago, carrying with him the hopes of 250 million Pakistani people.

Detailing his experience in China, he said what he gained had far exceeded his initial expectations. “First, I have acquired space flight skills. The management, staff and instructors at the Astronaut Centre of China have provided me with comprehensive training […] Day after day, I take another step upward.”

Moreover, he continued, “I have learned the Chinese language and developed a friendship with my Chinese colleagues. Now, I can communicate with my Chinese crewmates in Chinese.”

“We trained side by side, and our friendship embodies the friendship between China and Pakistan: higher than the mountains, deeper than the oceans, sweeter than honey and as vast as outer space.”

He said he had come to deeply appreciate the meaning of Chinese President Xi Jinping’s words: “Exploring the vast universe, developing the space industry and building China into a space power is our unremitting pursuit of the space dream.”

“Now, this dream belongs to me and also belongs to Pakistan,” he said, adding that it would become a reality through the Shenzhou-24 space mission.

He said he believed that “what comes after this flight matters more than the flight itself”.

“This mission is not a finish line; it is a launch pad. And I hope the next chapter of China-Pakistan space cooperation will grow from one astronaut, one flight to multiple joint flights, to the Moon, Mars and beyond.”

Ali noted that 2026 marked the 75th anniversary of China-Pakistan space cooperation, as well as the 75th anniversary of diplomatic relations between the two countries.

“Together with my Chinese crewmates, I will show that this friendship is real — not only on paper, but also in orbit.”

Addressing Pakistanis, he said: “I now feel the true weight of the words of our Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, when he told me that we were about to write a new chapter in Pakistan’s history. I want to assure you that I am not flying for myself. I carry the name of Pakistan on my shoulder, the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis.

“To every child who is watching while I am in orbit, just look up at the sky and remember that I am up there with your dreams.”

In his message to his family, he said: “I want to say that I will travel farther from home than ever before. But I promise you one thing: whenever I look back at the Earth for the first time, I will always search for the light of our home before I look at anything else. No matter how far I fly, we will still share the same Moon, and I will still be looking back at the same Earth.”

He then thanked his teammates, those who supported him, his instructors, scientists, engineers, Pakistan’s space agency Suparco and CMSA.

“You are the reason that I am here today, and no matter who flies, we have already made history together,” he said.

Ali said the “two great nations of China and Pakistan have turned a pilot into an astronaut in a remarkably short span of time. Now it is time to show this great achievement, innovation and send the world a message that we work hand in hand for the peaceful utilisation of space”.

Separately, China Daily posted a video of Ali saying that his planned one-year training had been compressed to seven months.

“That was the biggest challenge for me when I came here. But the real challenge, honestly speaking, was mastering the Chinese language. Learning the Chinese language is the key to the hearts of Chinese people,” he added.

Ali said he had read about several astronauts saying that whenever they looked at Earth from outer space, they did not see any borders or “separation between human beings; it is one humanity”.

He said he would be taking handwritten letters from his children and father on his journey “as a great source of inspiration”.

‘A moment that belongs to every Pakistani’

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif took to X to wish the crew his best for Wednesday’s liftoff.

“Tomorrow, Pakistan will look to the skies with anticipation, pride and deep gratitude,” he said, adding that as the Shenzhou-24 prepared to carry a Pakistani astronaut to Tiangong Space Station, the nation stood on the “threshold of a historic first”.

“It is a moment that belongs to every Pakistani,” he wrote and paid tribute to Suparco as well as academic institutions, scientists, the armed forces and national institutions whose efforts reflected “Pakistan’s growing capabilities and our determination to reach new frontiers in space exploration and scientific advancement”.

PM Shehbaz expressed gratitude to President Xi, stating that his “visionary” leadership had taken Pakistan-China friendship to an “unprecedented height”.

“This historic milestone is a powerful testament to the exceptional trust, mutual respect and enduring brotherhood that bind our two nations and peoples,” Shehbaz wrote, as he wished the crew his best for a safe and successful mission.

“Pakistan-China friendship is surpassing the heights of the Himalayas, in its journey to outer space.”

President Asif Ali Zardari also congratulated the nation on “historic” participation in the Chinese mission, calling it a “landmark achievement” and a “moment of immense national pride”, according to a statement issued by his office.

He said the mission reflected Pakistan’s “growing commitment to science, research and technological advancement”, lauding the role of Suparco, Pakistani scientists and China’s cooperation.

President Zardari expressed hope that the mission would open new horizons for Pakistan’s space programme and the scientific experiments undertaken during the mission would contribute to advancing research and knowledge.

“Pakistan’s participation in human spaceflight is not only about reaching space but also about harnessing its opportunities for research, learning and innovation. Citing a verse from national poet Allama Iqbal — Sitaron se aagay jahan aur bhi hain — he stressed that the mission should inspire Pakistan’s young generation to “pursue knowledge, innovation and scientific discovery with confidence and ambition”.

Planning Minister Iqbal also congratulated Ali and said the joint mission marked “the trust and iron brotherhood between Pakistan and China”.

Sharing a photo of himself from the press briefing in Jiayuguan, Ahsan posted on X: “Filled with immense pride and excitement as I witness history in the making.

“Watching our astronaut, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, take centre stage is a deeply moving moment. We had dared to dream big and laid the foundational blocks for Pakistan’s space and science ambitions. Today, seeing those dreams take flight and come true right before our eyes reaffirms that no frontier is beyond our reach when a nation commits to knowledge, innovation, and perseverance. The sky is no longer our limit—it is our launching pad.”

Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar congratulated the entire nation and Suparco as “Pakistan prepares to make history in space exploration and joint scientific advancement”.

“​With China’s Shenzhou-24 mission set for launch, a Pakistani astronaut, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, will soon enter orbit as the first international crew member aboard the Tiangong space station. This landmark moment speaks volumes about the strength of our all-weather strategic cooperative partnership.

“​My sincere wishes go out to all the astronauts on board for a smooth and successful mission. Long live Pakistan-China friendship!” he posted on X.

Information Minister Attaullah Tarar posted on X that a Pakistani astronaut going into space was a source of joy and pride for the entire nation.

Earlier in April, astronauts Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud were chosen from Pakistan to start training in China. Suparco and CMSA signed a cooperation agreement in February 2025, under which two Pakistanis were to train in China, with one joining a mission on the Tiangong space station operated by Beijing.

Eventually, Ali was chosen to join the Chinese mission.

Making the announcement, Iqbal wrote on X that he wanted to “honour both of our astronauts, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud, selected for final training. They were chosen from among Pakistan’s finest pilots and scientists. They trained in centrifuges and simulators, in emergency drills and in a new language. Both performed exceptionally well in training. Though Zeeshan Ali will fly tomorrow, both have already made Pakistan proud”.

PM Shehbaz had met the two in April and congratulated them on their selection. He said the space mission was a “matter of pride for the entire nation” and further commended their “round-the-clock efforts” in achieving the milestone.

PM Shehbaz had hoped that the space mission would “add new dimensions to the brotherly relationship between Pakistan and China”.

Beijing has invested heavily in its space programme in recent years — catching up with its main rival the United States — and aims to land astronauts on the Moon by 2030.

Pakistan is a natural partner, given the country’s decades-long, “all-weather” friendship. China is Pakistan’s largest trading partner and a major foreign investor.

China has been barred from participating in the International Space Station since 2011, when the US prohibited Nasa from collaborating with Chinese space agencies. Since then, Beijing has sought to build partnerships with other countries — including Pakistan — in its expanding space programme.

## Vocabulary

- **Orbital** — related to a path in space around a planet or star
- **Propellant** — fuel that creates the force to push something forward, like a rocket
- **Payload specialist** — a crew member on a space mission whose job is to manage experiments or equipment
