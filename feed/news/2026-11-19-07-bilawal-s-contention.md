> Why it matters: This is important for understanding the current power dynamics, parliamentary politics, and debates regarding federalism and governance in Pakistan.



Bilawal Bhutto Zardari's recent catharsis deserves a candid appraisal. His call for a political reset – at a presser in Karachi on Tuesday – is a welcome step. Such a reset necessitates a national dialogue involving all political forces, including the PTI - an outreach Bilawal gracefully hinted at despite their years of bitter rivalry. Also, it is hard to disagree with his contention that policies of exclusion, pursued over decades, have severely dented national interests. His acknowledgment that the PTI has been wronged and must be part of the solution is also spot on. However, Bilawal's outburst against the prevailing system has raised eyebrows, particularly since he and his party, the PPP, have been consistent beneficiaries of it. Thus, his assessment that the system has been "made to collapse" must be accompanied by an acceptance of responsibility. He cannot simply absolve himself of this burden or critique the status quo merely for political point-scoring; doing so is tantamount to having one's cake and eating it too. This criticism is echoed by the pushback from the PML-N. Defence Minister Khawaja Asif and other party leaders have reminded Bilawal that it was the collective wisdom of the coalition allies that produced these very policies. Thus, there is little point in walking away now, especially as the debate over creating new provinces or administrative units is underway. Asif went so far as to challenge Bilawal to come clean before assigning blame for the current decay, pointing out that Sindh continues to lag in development despite being ruled by the PPP for decades. Bilawal's remarks have come at a time when proposals are on the table to divide Sindh, alongside other provinces. There is little doubt that the Bhuttos are reluctant to see their influence in their traditional political bastion diluted by its administrative division. Yet Pakistan, mired in poor governance and an imbalance in resource distribution, is in dire need of more provinces. Devolving power into smaller administrative units is a common practice worldwide to ensure better service delivery. It is time to kickstart a serious debate on how to achieve this.

## Key facts

- ★ Bilawal Bhutto Zardari has called for a political reset involving all forces including the PTI.
- ★ The PPP has been in power in Sindh for several decades.
- ★ Discussions are currently ongoing regarding the creation of new provinces or administrative units.
- ★ The PML-N has challenged the PPP to reflect on its own performance in Sindh while critiquing the wider political system.

## Vocabulary

- **catharsis** — The process of releasing strong or repressed emotions.
- **tantamount** — Virtually equivalent to something, usually something bad.
