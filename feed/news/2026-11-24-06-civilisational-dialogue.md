> Why it matters: This highlights the state of Pakistan-India relations, the role of multilateral forums in diplomacy, and the potential for dialogue despite strained ties, crucial for Pakistan Affairs and International Relations.



WHENEVER representatives of Pakistan and India meet, even in multilateral settings, there is hope for a rekindling of ties. This is so because bilateral engagement is currently close to zero, mainly because of New Delhi’s rigidity. But considering last year’s armed conflict between both states, and the ensuing toxic atmosphere in South Asia, it is a welcome development that Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik travelled to New Delhi recently for the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum.

This was a multilateral meeting and there were no great expectations of a bilateral breakthrough attached to the visit. But the fact that it took place at all despite the state of ties is significant. Sometimes at such events, behind closed doors, representatives of both states can discuss small steps to improve relations.

Mr Malik brought up the issue of climate change threatening the whole region, while highlighting the common civilisational bonds that tie South Asia and Eurasia together — bonds which have unfortunately become hostage to the rigidity of some, particularly in the subcontinent. The minister also deserves praise for deftly handling pointed questions from the Indian media about ‘terrorism’ — apparently New Delhi’s favourite refrain when it comes to Pakistan — as well as the IWT. Mr Malik said that he was in India for an SCO event; when the Indians would call him to discuss terrorism and other irritants, he would address these specifically, as Pakistan also had concerns.

Though there are no signs of the deep freeze in bilateral relations thawing anytime soon, engagements such as these can help bring down the temperature, and lower confrontational rhetoric.

The fact is there is no viable alternative to peace in South Asia, and only through a structured dialogue can both states address their concerns satisfactorily, and work towards a more peaceful future for all.

Of course the Indian side must stop issuing threats to Pakistan, and using adversarial language. While Pakistan has defended itself against Indian aggression in the past, and will do so again if threatened, the subcontinent’s people deserve to live in peace, not eternal conflict.

When India is ready to engage Pakistan in a civil, frank and respectful dialogue, it will find this country ready to respond.
