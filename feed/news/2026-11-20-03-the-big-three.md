> Why it matters: Provides crucial arguments and examples for International Relations papers regarding great-power diplomacy, multipolarity, and strategic stability.



The Shenzhen summit of the Big Three has helped ease the nervousness prevailing amid two simultaneous wars – in Iran and Ukraine. Although no policy direction emerged from the APEC moot in the Chinese city, Presidents Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin carried the day by showing far-sightedness over realpolitik. They reportedly agreed to disagree over conflicting interests in Europe and the Middle East while stopping short of airing their concerns publicly. More reassuringly, they pledged not to exercise the nuclear option - even in extreme adversity - and called for reforms to the United Nations. The troika managed to make headlines even though they met without a formal agenda and dispersed without sowing any seeds of animosity. While the salient features of the 'peace declaration' are yet to be revealed, it seems they have agreed to lower the threshold of volatility over the Strait of Hormuz and the Eurasian heartland. That is no mean achievement, and Trump was right in saying that "we are really making history." For many, it evoked a sense of d&eacute;j&agrave; vu reminiscent of Yalta 1945, where the then-victor powers sought to assert their spheres of influence while resolving not to go to war again. Shenzhen, likewise, made one thing categorically clear: the world is moving towards multipolarity. The APEC summit will long be remembered for a series of gestures that the three leaders exhibited with remarkable largesse. For the first time, Xi was seen receiving a guest at the airport - apparently as a quid pro quo for his welcome at Andrews Air Base - while Trump called Putin his "friend" and refrained from commenting on Iran. The impulsive octogenarian leader also remarked that "no peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement." That was astute diplomacy in seamless coordination. The trilateral assurance that supply chain disruptions must end brought a sigh of relief, as global production is projected to dip by 1.7 per cent. The G-3 must sustain this momentum and ensure that belligerence comes to a permanent end.

## Key facts

- ★ The leaders of the United States, China, and Russia met informally during the APEC summit in Shenzhen.
- ★ The three leaders pledged not to use the nuclear option even under conditions of extreme adversity.
- ★ The summit produced calls for institutional reforms to the United Nations.
- ★ The leaders agreed to reduce volatility over the Strait of Hormuz and the Eurasian heartland amid simultaneous conflicts in Ukraine and Iran.

## Vocabulary

- **multipolarity** — an international system where power is shared among several major states
- **realpolitik** — politics based on practical and material factors rather than moral principles
- **largesse** — generosity in spirit, attitude, or gifts
