> Why it matters: This story addresses important issues of governance, human rights, education policy, and youth welfare in Pakistan.



Curbing the rise of vaping and substance abuse among youth is an urgent necessity. Thus, the Punjab government's decision to search students' bags outside educational institutions across the province is commendable in intent, yet what is condemnable is the use of the Crime Control Department (CCD) - a specialised crime-fighting police unit - to ensure students do not carry cigarettes, vapes and other intoxicating substances. This is all the more troubling because the CCD has earned a fearsome reputation in just over a year of existence, having been set up only last year. Human rights bodies, including the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (HRCP), have accused it of extrajudicial killings and bypassing due process. Furthermore, petitions in the Lahore High Court have cited allegations of illegal detentions and misconduct. Disciplining students inside academic spaces should be the job of educators themselves, not police. Routine checks can easily be handled by class teachers, student proctors or school administrators without turning campus gates into security checkpoints. It goes without saying that subjecting teenagers and pre-teens to searches by an infamous police wing carries psychological consequences. Treating adolescents like hardened suspects runs the risk of eroding their trust in authority and fostering resentment rather than genuine behavioural change. Instead of feeling safe in spaces meant for learning, children may feel intimidated, humiliated and stigmatised. To genuinely protect students from substance abuse, the authorities must tackle the problem at its root. First, law enforcement should focus on prosecuting shopkeepers and illicit networks selling vapes and narcotics near campuses. Second, institutions must introduce confidential counseling and awareness workshops led by psychologists. A drug-free campus is built through mentorship, parental engagement and supply-side enforcement - never through the heavy hand of a feared police force.

- ★ The Punjab government decided to search students' bags at educational institutions.
- ★ The Crime Control Department (CCD) was established last year.
- ★ The Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (HRCP) accused the CCD of extrajudicial killings.
