> Why it matters: It marks the first time a state has been found guilty of genocide by the International Court of Justice, setting a crucial legal precedent.



NEARLY a decade after the world watched Myanmar’s junta conduct a brutal campaign of extermination and displacement against the Muslim Rohingya minority, the International Court of Justice has ruled that the regime is guilty of genocide. It took a small African state — The Gambia — to lodge a case at the ICJ against Myanmar. Today, because of The Gambia’s courage and moral clarity, this verdict has made history as it is the first time a state has been held guilty of genocide. While calling out a blood-soaked campaign for what it was, the ruling also sets a legal precedent for building a strong case against another genocidal state: Israel. To recall, in 2017 the Myanmar military unleashed massive violence against the Rohingya in Rakhine state, with thousands killed, including over 2,000 children under five, rampant sexual attacks, while hundreds of thousands of Rohingya were forced to flee. The Rohingya have long faced discrimination, with the regime denying most citizenship. The junta claims it had conducted ‘counterinsurgency operations’, a defence rejected by the ICJ.

Though the ruling grants the Rohingya a moral victory, it is difficult to see the community returning to their homes in Rakhine in an atmosphere of safety, as the junta is currently engaged in a civil war there with Arakan rebels. However, Myanmar should pay compensation, as ordered by the court, to the victims or their survivors, punish those involved in the atrocities, and grant the community full citizenship. The ICJ ruling, legal experts say, is also likely to strengthen South Africa’s genocide case against Israel for what it has done — and is doing — in Gaza. Many scholars of genocide, including UN experts, are convinced that Israel has conducted a genocide in the occupied Palestinian territory. Sadly, just as the international community remained silent while the Rohingya were butchered, a similar silence — punctured by ineffectual criticism — was seen at the height of Israel’s murderous campaign in Gaza, until last year’s ceasefire. The Rohingya have finally received justice after a nightmarish ordeal; the people of Palestine now await justice. Israel needs to be brought to book for its bloodletting in Gaza and the West Bank, while all those foreign states that aided and abetted its crimes must similarly be held to account.

## Key facts

- ★ The International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar's regime is guilty of genocide against the Rohingya minority.
- ★ The landmark legal challenge was lodged at the ICJ by The Gambia.
- ★ The 2017 military assault in Rakhine state resulted in thousands of deaths, including more than 2,000 children under five.
- ★ The ICJ ordered Myanmar to pay compensation to victims and grant full citizenship rights to the Rohingya.
- ★ Legal experts note the verdict strengthens South Africa's genocide petition against Israel regarding Gaza.

## Vocabulary

- **extermination** — killing or destroying a group completely
- **counterinsurgency** — military action taken to defeat rebels or insurgents
