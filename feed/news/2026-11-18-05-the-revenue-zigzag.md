> Why it matters: It explains the critical fiscal relationship between the federation and provinces, providing insights into constitutional provisions and current economic challenges.



A familiar jugglery of figures is underway between the federation and the provinces. Struggling to meet its revenue targets - and burdened by the 7th NFC Award, which hands 57.5% of net federal taxes to the provinces - Islamabad has struck a deal of convenience with the federating units. While the rationale behind this provincial largesse remains unclear, a staggering Rs1.035 trillion in cash grants will flow back to the federal exchequer. Even Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa, long at odds with the Centre over its withheld dues, has agreed to pitch in Rs157 billion annually. This figure also absorbs the Rs17.8 billion previously deducted at source from the reluctant province's NFC share. The agreement signed earlier this month is being seen as a win-win proposition under Article 164 of the Constitution, which enables cash grants to the Centre. The point, however, is that it is a zigzag under duress, as the present political setup lacks credibility. K-P, under Governor's Rule, is obviously not in a position to put up any resistance, unlike the suspended two-thirds majority government of the PTI. Chief Minister Sohail Afridi had moved the Federal Constitutional Court claiming Rs964 billion from the Federal Consolidated Fund regarding the continued extension of the 7th NFC Award and the merger of the erstwhile FATA into the province. The additional funds sought by K-P were to the tune of over Rs1.6 trillion. The accord can only be seen as a respite for a cash-starved Islamabad. It is also an open secret that the federation hopes to claw back a bigger slice of revenue from the provinces - a move that would require amending the constitutional protection under Article 160(3A). The Centre says it needs money for expenditures on security and development projects. Rather than opting for credible austerity and belt-tightening to curb widespread leakages, the decision to bank on the provinces is an ill-advised initiative that contravenes the provincial autonomy exercised under the 18th Amendment. All that is needed is a new, non-partisan revenue distribution formula based on the provinces' human development indicators and revenue-generation capability.

## Key facts

- ★ The 7th NFC Award mandates that 57.5% of net federal taxes are transferred to the provinces.
- ★ Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa has agreed to contribute Rs157 billion annually to the federal exchequer.
- ★ Article 164 of the Constitution permits the federal government to grant funds to provinces or vice versa.
- ★ The former provincial government of K-P claimed Rs964 billion from the Federal Consolidated Fund.
- ★ The federal government is reportedly considering amendments to Article 160(3A) to alter revenue-sharing mechanisms.

## Vocabulary

- **Largesse** — Generous distribution of money or gifts.
- **Exchequer** — The national treasury or public funds.
- **Duress** — Threats or pressure used to force someone to do something.
