> Why it matters: This story offers insights into Pakistan-India relations, diplomatic challenges, and regional forums like the SCO.



The SCO Civilizational Dialogue forum held in New Delhi will long be remembered for bullying a guest from Pakistan. Federal Minister for Climate Change Dr Musadik Malik faced relentless attempts to grill him by the Indian media on every issue on earth, except the subject for which he was there. That spoke of the belligerence in the air and the level of intolerance that has crept into the Indian media. Kudos to Dr Malik for keeping his cool while engaging a hostile audience with his bilingual retort, which also featured poetry to lighten the mood. He exhibited his articulation by confining himself to the bare minimum of words while responding to provocative questions. The minister was apt when he said any dialogue between Pakistan and India on "terrorism" must be "based on evidence," resting his country's case on reason. Dr Malik's three-day official visit came after a break of more than three years, since then-Foreign Minister Bilawal Bhutto Zardari toured Goa to participate in a similar SCO moot in May 2023. Later, the Pahalgam attack of April 2025 led to a further downgrading of diplomatic ties, with high-level engagement entirely absent since the arch-foes engaged in a four-day military confrontation in May 2025. With diplomatic relations at their lowest ebb and consular facilitation non-existent, it was a rare opportunity for India to extend due courtesy to its high-profile guest from Pakistan and kick-start a new episode of meaningful interaction. That was sadly not the case, as vendetta and hubris ruled the roost. Dr Malik, nonetheless, was able to make some strides as he expressed hope that bilateral dialogue would resume at the earliest. He was spot-on when he said he would prefer to answer loaded queries concerning interstate ties at an opportune moment when specifically called upon to do so. That was clarity at work, intended not to stir any unnecessary controversy. His remarks must serve as a preamble for a meaningful dialogue on climate change: "GLOFs and glaciers are blind, they do not know whether to fall on the right or the left." That is the evolving shared threat that both neighbours must confront immediately.

## Key facts

- ★ Federal Minister for Climate Change Dr Musadik Malik visited New Delhi for the SCO Civilizational Dialogue forum.
- ★ The visit was the first high-level engagement between Pakistan and India in over three years.
- ★ Then-Foreign Minister Bilawal Bhutto Zardari attended a similar SCO moot in Goa in May 2023.
- ★ Diplomatic ties worsened after the Pahalgam attack in April 2025.
- ★ A four-day military confrontation occurred in May 2025.
- ★ Dr Malik stated any dialogue on 'terrorism' must be 'based on evidence'.

- **retort** — a sharp, quick, or witty reply
- **hubris** — excessive pride or self-confidence
