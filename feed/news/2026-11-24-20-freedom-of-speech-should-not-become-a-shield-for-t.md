> Why it matters: This addresses the ongoing debate on balancing freedom of expression with national security and public order in the digital age, a key governance and media issue.



Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi on Tuesday asserted that freedom of speech should not become a shield for those who cross “red lines”.

He made the remarks while addressing a seminar organised by the Sustainable Development Policy Institute (SDPI).

At the outset of his speech, the minister said that there were four “red lines” for the state: terrorism, hate speech, incitement to violence and sexual exploitation of children.

“On these four points, there is no compromise. Pakistan has paid for terrorism in blood. As interior minister, I have attended the funerals of martyrs. And very often, the hate that killed them began on a mobile phone,” he said.

He alleged that only 47 per cent of the complaints made by the Counter Terrorism Department were acted upon by social media platforms.

“This is not about technology, this is about people. We are not saying that free speech must be curtailed […] journalists must report, citizens must question, dissent must be heard, including against the government, but freedom of speech can never become a shield for those who cross these red lines,” he said.

He went on to say that Pakistan had “limited manpower”, resources and technology. Giving the example of Punjab, he said that the province had a population of 140 million against a strength of 61 people in the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA).

“And these 61 people include drivers, clerks and officers. We cannot do this alone. That is why we have established a Social Media Protection Regulatory Authority,” he said.

The minister called for moving from policy towards enforcement.

“To begin with [social media] platforms, OTT services and major content creators must register with the authority. Registration is not a licence to silence anyone, it tells us who is accountable when something goes wrong,” he said.

He said that every social media platform “must name an empowered representative for Pakistan, who can actually take decisions”.

“Similarly, citizens need one complaint channel they can track. We are moving all NCCIA complaints onto the Pak ID app and the authority is building a single fully tracked complaint system,” he said.

Naqvi then went on to recount a recent town hall meeting, where he met five girls who were on the verge of ending their lives due to someone about to release “sensitive material” on social media platforms.

“We don’t have any pathway for approaching those platforms. It’s their responsibility to react immediately to save these innocent lives,” he said.

The minister said that the social media authority would verify whether action had been taken or not.

“Finally, enforcement will be step by step: engagement, then notice, then penalties. Blocking a whole [social media] platform will remain a last resort … our request will be clear, lawful and open to review,” he said.

“The state should not feel helpless. Yet, we see terrorist groups still recruiting, still raising terrorist funds, still spreading fear on these very platforms,” he said.

Giving an example, Naqvi said that the Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA) was a declared terrorist organisation, yet “many of their officials are still using these platforms”.

“If it is a declared [terrorist] organisation, then who allows them to move around and use all these platforms? Someone needs to stop [them]. This is our request,” he said.

“If you are not doing it, what meaning should we take? If it is declared by your country that this organisation is a blacklisted organisation, is a terrorist organisation and then you don’t take action, there’s a big question mark,” he said. He added that the government was focusing on the capacity building of the NCCIA.

“Today, I extend an open hand to all these platforms. Let us sit together, not across the table. Operate globally, be accountable globally and never let Pakistani citizens feel unheard,” he concluded.

- **Accountable** — required or expected to justify actions or decisions
