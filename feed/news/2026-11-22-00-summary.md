**Chinaman or doosra** (Dawn · Shahzad Sharjeel)

Pakistani decision-makers often look admiringly at China's centralised political model to deliver rapid economic progress. This top-down authority appeals to leaders who want to take difficult actions without facing multiparty resistance. However, trying to copy such centralized control ignores the realities of Pakistan's distinct constitutional and societal makeup.

- ★ China and India are two large neighbouring economies that started modern development around the same time as Pakistan and expanded significantly faster.
- ★ China operates a centralised political system, whereas India and Pakistan follow multiparty systems.
- ★ China expanded its global economic reach through mass export manufacturing of affordable consumer goods.

**Reining it in** (Dawn · Editorial)

President Asif Ali Zardari held a meeting with the prime minister and the interior minister to defuse coalition tensions over the new provinces debate. The interaction followed aggressive public speeches by PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari that signaled potential fractures in the ruling alliance. An official statement noted that both sides agreed to address their differences through dialogue. While direct conflict has been avoided for now, the PML-N has yet to state its official position on administrative restructuring.

- ★ President Asif Ali Zardari met with the prime minister and interior minister to resolve political tensions.
- ★ The coalition dispute centered on opposing positions regarding the creation of new provinces.
- ★ A readout from the Presidency stated that all outstanding issues would be resolved via dialogue.
- ★ The PML-N has kept its official stance on administrative restructuring unannounced.

**Theatre control** (Dawn · Editorial)

The Punjab government enacted the Punjab Dramatic and Theatrical Performances Bill 2026 to monitor and censor commercial theatre. The law mandates prior content screening for obscenity and requires organizers to obtain approval fifteen days before any performance. The provincial minister for information and culture defended the law as an attempt to make stage shows family-friendly. Critics maintain that heavy-handed censorship ignores the economic difficulties of artists and calls instead for genuine cultural patronage.

- ★ The Punjab Dramatic and Theatrical Performances Bill 2026 was passed to regulate local theatre productions.
- ★ The bill mandates official permission 15 days before events and requires script reviews for obscenity or inflammatory content.
- ★ The provincial minister for information and culture stated the law aims to regulate rather than prohibit performances.
- ★ Indigenous Punjabi theatre draws from folk traditions but faces commercial pressure due to an absence of state funding.

[Read it at Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2046080/theatre-control)

**Healthcare in limbo** (The Express Tribune · editorial)

The Sindh HealthCare Commission has functioned without a regular chairman for 18 months, and its board's tenure has also lapsed. This leadership void undermines the body's duty to inspect medical facilities and combat unlicensed practitioners. Discussions in the Sindh Assembly underscored the commission's limited presence outside major urban centers like Karachi. Leaving rural regions without adequate oversight leaves millions vulnerable to substandard healthcare.

- ★ The Sindh HealthCare Commission has operated without a chairman for 18 months.
- ★ The tenure of the commission's governing board has expired.
- ★ The commission is officially tasked with establishing standards for healthcare facilities and eradicating quackery in Sindh.
