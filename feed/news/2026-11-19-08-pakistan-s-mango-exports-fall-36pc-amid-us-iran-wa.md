> Why it matters: This illustrates the tangible impact of regional geopolitical conflicts on Pakistan’s agricultural exports and foreign exchange earnings.



Pakistan’s mango exports fell 36 per cent this year as the US-Iran war disrupted shipping routes and drove up freight costs, industry figures showed on Wednesday.

According to All Pakistan Fruit and Vegetables Exporters Association, the country exported 78,000 tonnes of mangoes this year, compared with 123,000 tons last year.

Exports to the Middle East, a major market for Pakistani mangoes, dropped from 36,000 tonnes last year to 14,500 tonnes this year, the association said.

Waheed Ahmed, head of the association, told Anadolu that the ongoing conflict had reduced the country’s mango export earnings from approximately $100 million to around $60m.

He cited the months-long near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a key shipping route, and rising freight rates as major factors behind the decline, particularly in exports to Middle Eastern countries.

“The freight rate that was $1,000-1,400 before the US-Iran war, now stands at $8,000. Secondly, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz also increased the shipping period , which affected the exports of perishable items like mangoes,” he said.

Ahmed said the United Arab Emirates and Oman were the main Middle Eastern destinations for Pakistani mangoes, particularly the internationally acclaimed Chaunsa variety, followed by Saudi Arabia.

Kuwait and Qatar also import significant quantities of Pakistani mangoes, according to the association.

Mango exports to neighboring Afghanistan were also disrupted by frequent border closures amid strained relations between the two countries, Ahmed said.

Pakistan produced 1.9m metric tonnes of mangoes annually until 2018, ranking sixth globally behind India, China, Thailand, Indonesia and Mexico.

However, production has declined over the past four years, with the association attributing the drop to global warming.

Climate change has reduced Pakistan’s total mango production by 35pc, according to the association.

## Key facts

- ★ Mango exports fell by 36 percent this year.
- ★ Total exports dropped from 123,000 tonnes last year to 78,000 tonnes this year.
- ★ Freight rates surged from $1,000–$1,400 to $8,000 due to the US-Iran conflict.
- ★ Export earnings from mangoes declined from approximately $100 million to $60 million.
- ★ Pakistan historically ranked sixth in global mango production.
