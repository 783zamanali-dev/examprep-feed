The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) says it has uncovered a betting and money-laundering network with an estimated transaction exposure of up to Rs1 trillion. This is a staggering example of the scale of the problem. The network allegedly funneled gambling proceeds through commercial banks, microfinance institutions, mobile wallets, shell companies and 'mule accounts', including one settlement account that alone received Rs3.72 billion in less than two years. The NCCIA froze roughly Rs1 billion and $7 million in USDT. Scams have also cost Pakistanis dearly. Over 227,000 citizens have been scammed in the past three years, losing a combined Rs8.32 billion, with complaints rising 40% last year. The vast majority of domestic hits were driven by fake bank calls and WhatsApp job scams. However, the actual number of cases is likely much higher. A study by Visa found roughly one in two Pakistanis experienced online financial fraud in 2024, and annual losses from digital fraud may reach $9 billion about 2.5% of GDP. Many scams also use mobile wallets and other products which may have exploitable "know-your-customer" loopholes. A typical banking scam involves a 'bank official' demanding payment to make a seemingly bigger - but previously unknown - financial issue disappear. Not only is this scam relatively easy, but it also does not require massive upfront investment. That is why, with only about 350 dedicated cybercrime investigators handling over 160,000 cases every year, enforcement is still a challenge. And as long as the risk and penalties for being caught remain low, scammers will treat it as a cost of doing business and just keep reopening shop. Without heavy penalties and better enforcement, there is no incentive to discourage people from indulging in scams, especially when scammers know how easily an arrested person can be made to go home.

## Key facts

- ★ The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) uncovered a betting and money-laundering network exposed to up to Rs1 trillion.
- ★ Authorities froze roughly Rs1 billion and $7 million in USDT associated with illicit networks.
- ★ Over 227,000 Pakistani citizens lost a combined Rs8.32 billion to scams across three years.
- ★ A Visa study revealed that annual digital financial fraud losses in Pakistan may reach $9 billion, or around 2.5% of GDP.
- ★ Only about 350 dedicated cybercrime investigators currently handle more than 160,000 cases each year in Pakistan.

## Vocabulary

- **exposure** — the total financial risk or amount of money involved in a scheme
- **mule accounts** — bank accounts used by criminals to receive and transfer illicit money
