> Why it matters: This relates to the economic potential of natural resources and the geopolitical implications of energy security and foreign partnerships.



Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar on Friday said that the estimated worth of minerals, hydrocarbons and other natural resources in Pakistan was between $8 trillion and $10tr.

And this was the “most conservative estimate”, he said while addressing a ceremony held in Karachi to mark the arrival of a Turkish seismic research vessel, Oruc Reis.

The vessel, which arrived on Thursday, will be used for offshore oil and gas exploration in Pakistan.

Dar began his address by welcoming the leadership and professionals of the Turkish Petroleum, as well as the captain, crew and seismic research team of Oruc Reis, and termed the occasion a “historic moment”.

He noted that Oruc Reis brought “Turkish scientific expertise together with the experience and capabilities of our national energy companies to advance exploration in Pakistan’s offshore domain”.

Dar added, “Every successful journey of exploration begins with knowledge. The data gathered by this vessel may deepen our understanding of the geological structures beneath the seabed and provide a stronger basis for Pakistan’s future exploration plan.”

He described offshore exploration as “complex, capital-intensive and inherently uncertain”.

And a seismic survey could not guarantee a commercial discovery, he continued. “But no discovery can be made without seismic and exploration,” he stressed, adding that today, Pakistan was taking that step with “confidence, responsibility and the prayers of our people”.

He said Pakistan sought a long-term energy partnership with Turkiye, encompassing offshore exploration, minerals and renewable energy.

“You all have witnessed three memorandums of understanding signed between the relevant Turkish and Pakistani agencies.

“We want Pakistani geologists and engineers to work very closely with their Turkish counterparts. We want our universities and research institutions to develop partnerships in marine, geoscience and offshore engineering,” he said.

He assured “our Turkish partners and brothers” that the government of Pakistan would extend every appropriate facilitation to the “very important mission” to be undertaken by Oruc Reis.

“All offshore activities must observe the highest standards of maritime safety and environmental protection. Our seas are an economic asset, but they are also a national trust. Development and environmental stewardship must proceed together,” he stressed.

Dar further said that the Turkish energy minister told him that the seismic mission would take around five to six months. “I said, you won’t be allowed to leave unless you give us good news of a real discovery,” he added.

He said he would come across people who described Pakistan as a country in a debt trap.

He went on to say the estimated value of the country’s natural resources, including mines and minerals, hydrocarbons, gemstones and rare earth materials, far exceeded its debt burden.

“Sometimes people talk about Pakistan being in debt, being a heavily indebted country, or being in a debt-trap; I always negate that,” he said, adding that Pakistan was endowed with abundant natural resources.

He thanked the Turkish government and people for standing alongside Pakistan and described the arrival of the Oruc Reis as another example of the two countries’ partnership.

Pakistan, Turkiye energy cooperation enters ‘new phase’

Meanwhile, Pakistan and Turkiye are targeting the start of drilling by the fourth quarter of 2027, following the seismic survey of the Indus and Makran offshore basins.

The announcement was made by Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik and his Turkish counterpart Alparslan Bayraktar at Thursday’s ceremony.

The vessel will collect three-dimensional seismic data across 670 square kilometres in the Eastern Offshore Indus-C Block over approximately two months, with analysis expected to take another six months.

The Turkish Petroleum Overseas Company (TPOC), a subsidiary of Turkish Petroleum Corporation (TPAO), is the operator of the Indus-C Block with a 25 per cent working interest. Pakistan Petroleum Limited holds 35pc, Oil and Gas Development Company Limited 20pc and Mari Energies the remaining 20pc.

Addressing the ceremony on Thursday, Malik said the arrival of the vessel was more than a routine port call.

“This is more than the arrival of a vessel. It is the moment when agreements become actual projects, and when a shared vision begins its journey from paper to the seabed,” he said.

Speaking at the ceremony, Bayraktar said licensing processes had been completed for five exploration blocks, comprising three offshore and two onshore sites, and field operations had begun.

Bayraktar said Pakistan’s energy cooperation with Turkiye had entered a new phase.

He said the two countries had signed several energy agreements in Islamabad last year, paving the way for a partnership between TPAO and Pakistan’s national energy companies Oil and Gas Development Company Limited (OGDCL), PPL, and Mari Energies.

“We are two nations with a history built on shared values and a deep-rooted brotherhood. Today, we have come together to take a historic step that will further strengthen this friendship and shape the energy future of both countries,” he said.

He said Oruc Reis Pakistan had a 19-day voyage to reach Karachi to explore the hydrocarbon potential of the Indus and Makran basins.

“Based on the data we collect, we aim to begin drilling operations at the designated sites next year,” Bayraktar said, adding that exploration activities could contribute to the development and energy security of both countries.

Pakistan has not auctioned offshore blocks for exploration since 2006-07 for various reasons, and previous exploration activities could not yield successful results in the offshore basin.

The block is estimated to contain 1.5-2 trillion cubic feet of gas, according to PPL’s 2D and 3D surveys.

Malik also said Pakistan’s offshore basins hold significant geological potential, but large areas have remained underexplored.

“What lies beneath these waters cannot be known through expectation alone. It requires science, technology, sustained investment and, above all, the courage to explore,” he said at the ceremony.

He said that under the leadership of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, the government had taken material steps to revive offshore exploration. Last year, after a gap of 18 years, Pakistan launched a competitive offshore bid round, receiving bids for 23 blocks covering approximately 53,510 square kilometres.

Turkiye’s participation, he said, holds particular significance. Through agreements covering three offshore and two onshore blocks, cooperation in petroleum exploration has expanded substantially.

“The arrival of this vessel takes our partnership from promise to execution. The seismic data it acquires will give us a clearer picture of the subsurface, reduce geological and drilling uncertainty, and form the basis of the future exploration programme,” the minister said.

He expressed hope that the data acquisition and interpretation exercise would be completed in six months, leading to the start of drilling operations.

Linking the project to the current energy security environment, the minister said Pakistan’s energy security is inseparable from economic security.

Welcoming the captain and crew of the Oruç Reis, Malik said they had brought not only an advanced research vessel, but a visible symbol of trust between the two nations.

PM calls for bilateral cooperation in energy, mines and minerals

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif stressed the need for enhanced bilateral cooperation between Pakistan and Turkiye in the fields of mines and minerals, renewable energy and others, while hosting a dinner in honour of Turkish Minister of Energy and Natural Resources Alparslan Bayraktar and his delegation at the PM House.

According to the Prime Minister’s Office (PMO), he said Pakistan’s relations Türkiye were rooted in “historic ties, longstanding friendship, mutual trust, brotherhood and shared interests”.

He thanked Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan for sending Oruc Reis to Pakistan and for Türkiye’s cooperation in the offshore oil and gas exploration project, welcoming the growing cooperation between Pakistan and Türkiye in the energy sector.

He said Pakistan wanted to further expand cooperation in the energy sector by benefiting from Türkiye’s experience and technical expertise.

Apart from offshore oil and gas exploration, there were vast opportunities to enhance bilateral cooperation in minerals and renewable energy, PM Shehbaz added.

The exploration of potential oil and gas reserves in Pakistan’s maritime areas was an “important step” towards strengthening the country’s energy security and promoting economic development, the prime minister said.

Bayraktar thanked the prime minister for the warm welcome and exemplary hospitality extended to him and his delegation in Pakistan.

He said the arrival of Oruc Reis in Pakistan marked the beginning of a new era of energy cooperation between the two countries.

Türkiye was committed to utilising its technical expertise, scientific experience and engineering capabilities in cooperation with Pakistan, Bayraktar said, adding that the scientific data collected through the project would help determine the prospects for future oil and gas exploration and drilling.

The Turkish delegation included Turkish Parliament member and Pakistan-Türkiye Friendship Group President Ali Sahin, Türkiye’s Ambassador to Pakistan Irfan Neziroglu, and senior officials of the Turkish Ministry of Energy.

DPM Dar, Petroleum Minister Malik, Federal Ministers Junaid Anwar Chaudhry, Attaullah Tarar and Sardar Awais Ahmed Khan Leghari; the Prime Minister’s Adviser Muhammad Ali, and Special Assistants Tariq Fatemi and Lt Gen (retd.) Muhammad Zafar Iqbal also attended the dinner.

During the ceremony, a shareholders’ agreement was also signed between representatives of Pakistan Mineral Development Corporation and Türkiye’s MTA International Company (MTAIC) to develop antimony in Pishin, Balochistan.

Another joint venture agreement was signed between Mari Minerals (Private) Limited and MTAIC of Turkiye to pursue exploration and development of copper, gold and associated mineral deposits in Chagai, Balochistan.

With this, a tripartite MoU was also signed between the Geological Survey of Pakistan, the General Directorate of Mineral Research and Exploration of Turkiye (MTA) and MTAIC for technical exchanges and capacity building in geological and mineral resource development.

The arrival of the Oruç Reis vessel follows the government’s revival of offshore exploration efforts after a gap of nearly two decades.

The Offshore Bid Round 2025 was launched in January last year, offering 40 offshore blocks in the Indus and Makran basins for petroleum exploration.

Pakistan’s offshore zone covers around 300,000 square kilometres, but only 18 wells have been drilled there since independence in 1947. A basin study by US firm DeGolyer and MacNaughton has identified significant untapped hydrocarbon potential in the country’s offshore basins.

## Key facts

- ★ Estimated value of Pakistan's natural resources is between $8 trillion and $10 trillion.
- ★ The Turkish vessel Oruc Reis will conduct offshore oil and gas seismic surveys.
- ★ The project involves three signed memorandums of understanding between Turkish and Pakistani agencies.
- ★ Offshore exploration is described by the government as capital-intensive and inherently uncertain.

## Vocabulary

- **Hydrocarbons** — Organic compounds containing hydrogen and carbon, typically found in oil and gas.
- **Seismic** — Relating to earth vibrations used to map underground geological structures.
