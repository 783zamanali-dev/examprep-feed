The UN's top climate official urged negotiators at the COP31 conference in Turkey on Saturday to move swiftly towards a deal and "avoid tactical delays" as the summit heads into its second and final week. The annual UN climate conference faces new challenges this year as a slew of crises from war to inflation push global warming down the list of priorities for world governments. "Each country has a view. Every country has a voice. Getting agreement on every word, every sentence is tough but vital work," Simon Stiell told the summit. "There is no collective success without compromise from every party. As we head into the second ?week, I urge parties to move swiftly to the hardest issues and to avoid tactical delays." Big fossil fuel producers and consumers at the talks are resisting any deal that would specify firm new targets or a shift away from oil, gas and coal, observers said, posing a challenge for summit host Turkey and lead negotiator Australia to land ?the kind of ambitious agreement climate advocates say is needed to address global warming. A UN report last month found the world is already on course to exceed the 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit) warming threshold, a limit scientists say should prevent the worst effects of climate change. Australia's COP31 President of Negotiations Chris Bowen said he expected a first draft of a deal text to emerge early next week. Turkey's electrification plan Turkey is striving to place a proposal to expand global electrification at the centre of an eventual deal, saying the plan will lead to faster emissions cuts by speeding the shift away from fossil fuel-powered cars, boilers, furnaces and other equipment. The proposal does not require the increased electricity use to come from renewable sources, however, disappointing observers and delegations that want countries to more clearly map out their transition away from fossil fuels. Major oil producers such as Saudi Arabia and Russia have traditionally resisted language constraining fossil fuels, while large developing economies such as India have opposed measures they say could limit their development and energy choices, a dynamic that has compromised the outcomes of past deals.

## Key facts

- ★ Turkey is serving as host of the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31), with Australia leading negotiations.
- ★ UN climate chief Simon Stiell called on delegates to resolve key disagreements without using procedural delays.
- ★ A recent UN climate assessment warned that global temperatures are already tracking to surpass the 1.5 degrees Celsius warming limit.
- ★ Host country Turkey proposed a global electrification initiative to accelerate the replacement of fossil-fuel machinery.

## Vocabulary

- **electrification** — the process of converting a system or device to operate on electric power
- **tactical** — actions carefully planned to gain a specific short-term military or political advantage
