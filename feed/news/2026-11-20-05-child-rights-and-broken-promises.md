World Children's Day is being observed today with the theme "My Day. My Rights. My Future", but for children in Pakistan, neither their rights nor their futures are secure. For many of the 120 million children who make up nearly half of the country's population, childhood is rarely a joyful time, and more often a nightmare fueled by deprivation, exploitation and violence. Beyond the ribbon-cuttings and events at luxury hotels, children all over the country continue to suffer due not only to the lack of several international-standard protections, but also to the abject failure to enforce most existing ones. More than 8.6 million children aged 5 to 17 are trapped in child labour, with 6.6 million confined to hazardous work that threatens their health and future. About four in every 10 children under five are stunted by chronic malnutrition. Meanwhile, up to 80 per cent of children experience violent discipline at home, while reported sexual crimes against children numbered 17,348 in 2025 alone. The numbers are so appalling that we did not even get to the fact that over 25 million children are out of school, the second-largest out-of-school population on earth. The recent launch of the first National Strategy to End Violence Against Children is a welcome acknowledgment of the crisis. But for the time being, it is just that - a strategy, and nothing more. Unless we bridge the gap between policy and implementation, millions more childhoods will be lost. But it is not just the government that is to blame. Children have been let down by society as a whole, from parents who thrash their children for sport, to community members who turn a blind eye due to cultural taboos, or religious leaders who abuse the children in their care. Some adults are lucky enough to remember the day their childhoods ended. If we can even get there, rather than forcing the harsh realities of life on our youngest citizens, it will at least be a tangible step in the right direction.

## Key facts

- ★ Pakistan has roughly 120 million children, making up nearly half of the national population.
- ★ More than 8.6 million children aged 5 to 17 are in child labour, with 6.6 million engaged in hazardous work.
- ★ About four in every 10 Pakistani children under the age of five suffer from chronic stunting.
- ★ Reported sexual crimes against children reached 17,348 cases in 2025 alone.
- ★ Pakistan has over 25 million out-of-school children, the second-largest out-of-school population in the world.
- ★ The country recently introduced its first National Strategy to End Violence Against Children.

## Vocabulary

- **abject** — complete, extreme, and utterly hopeless
- **stunted** — prevented from growing to normal physical size due to poor nutrition
