**PM Shehbaz stresses clean energy’s importance for Pakistan at Chashma-5 nuclear plant event** (Dawn · News Desk)

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif spoke at the dome placement ceremony of the Chashma-5 nuclear power plant near Mianwali. Dome placement marks the end of civil works and the start of internal installation. The 1,200MW plant, built with Chinese help, is expected to begin generating power in September 2030. The PM praised nuclear energy as clean and reliable and thanked China for its support.

- ★ Chashma-5 (C-5) near Mianwali will have a capacity of 1,200MW.
- ★ C-5 is expected to start generating electricity in September 2030.
- ★ C-5 is Pakistan's seventh nuclear plant built with China's help; its groundbreaking was in July 2023.
- ★ Pakistan's nuclear plants generate 3,530MW and avoid 15 million tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions a year.
- ★ Nuclear energy provides about 16.6pc of Pakistan's electricity generation from 8.6pc of installed capacity, rising to 26pc in winter.
- ★ Dr Raja Ali Raza Anwar is chairman of the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission.

**Lahore’s PM2.5 level eight times WHO guideline, report says** (Dawn · Imran Gabol)

A Pakistan Air Quality Initiative report found 16,714 likely crop-residue fires in Punjab between October 1 and November 9, while Lahore's average PM2.5 was eight times the WHO guideline. Pollution in Lahore was higher on days when winds passed over burning areas. The report said evidence did not support blaming Lahore's post-Diwali smog on Indian fireworks. Punjab's environment department reported improvement after anti-smog operations, and rain is forecast.

- ★ Lahore's average PM2.5 was 126 micrograms per cubic metre from October 1 to November 9, 2026, eight times the WHO guideline.
- ★ Satellites detected 18,825 fires across Pakistani Punjab, 89pc of them likely crop-residue burning.
- ★ Sheikhupura recorded the most fires (2,559), followed by Jhang, Okara, Hafizabad and Gujranwala.
- ★ Lahore's PM2.5 averaged 175 micrograms on days with air from burning areas, compared with 93 on other days.
- ★ Lahore's average AQI fell from 489 to 232 on one day after anti-smog operations, a drop of about 53pc.
