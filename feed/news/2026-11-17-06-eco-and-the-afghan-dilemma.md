> Why it matters: This touches upon regional security, Pakistan's foreign policy, and the stability of the Afghanistan-Pakistan border.



Pakistan has once again flagged its rightful concerns pertaining to security fissures emanating from across its western frontier. Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar reminded the 30th meeting of the ECO Council of Ministers in Islamabad that the Taliban regime has consistently failed to uphold its promises regarding the eradication of terror networks holed up inside Afghanistan. The non-seriousness of the reclusive administration can be gauged from the fact that no representative participated in the moot, confirming its defiance and contempt for regional concerns on such a vital issue. Kabul has been evading Islamabad's worries by openly pampering a host of non-state actors, including the outlawed TTP, out of political exigency. This has led to a sharp spike in terror activities inside Pakistan. Thus, Dar was spot on when he said no regional state is immune from this backlash and that it is incumbent upon Kabul to act before it is too late. The deputy PM told the regional foreign ministers that a stable Afghanistan is essential for realising geo-economic goals. Likewise, he had a point when he noted that regional integration is not merely about routes and connectivity, but about the trust that defines relationships among sovereign states. This is where Afghanistan is acting like a pariah state, drawing serious reservations from the international community over its conduct and credibility. The 10-member SCO recently reprimanded Kabul, reminding it that it is failing to act as a responsible state in upholding international law and bilateral commitments. Similarly, the UN and other rights bodies have time and again released dossiers detailing the terror networks simmering inside Afghanistan. It goes without saying that a peaceful Afghanistan would be a blessing for regional development and tranquility. The ECO has lagged behind in making meaningful strides over previous decades owing to a lack of consistency in regional collaboration, and it is now held back by the spectre of terrorism. It is time for regional stakeholders to put their heads together and ensure that Afghanistan is persuaded to change course before it slides further into an abyss of instability.

## Key facts

- ★ The 30th meeting of the ECO Council of Ministers was held in Islamabad.
- ★ Pakistan has expressed serious reservations over the Taliban regime's failure to dismantle terror networks like the TTP.
- ★ The 10-member Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) has recently reprimanded Kabul for failing to act as a responsible state.
- ★ Stable regional relations are defined by trust rather than just connectivity infrastructure.

- **fissures** — a state of incompatibility or disagreement
