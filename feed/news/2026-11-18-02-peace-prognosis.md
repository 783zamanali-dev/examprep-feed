AFGHAN foreign and domestic policies are a curious mix of tribalism, revanchism and ideological confusion that shun human rights and statecraft in favour of support for terrorist groups based on Afghan soil that pose a clear danger to regional peace. Unfortunately, all attempts at positive engagement with the Afghan Taliban by Pakistan as well as regional countries have been scuttled due to Kabul’s lack of willingness to rein in terrorist outfits operating under its benign gaze.

A belief has gained traction amongst the Afghan Taliban that their successful armed struggle against Nato/USA could also be emulated by their ideological comrades like Al Qaeda, IS-K, ETIM, the TTP etc in pursuit of their political objectives. Rooted in medieval notions of tribal honour and revenge, the regime offers a propitious environment for growth of terror networks. The flow of external funds into the Afghan regime’s coffers has encouraged the obduracy of the Taliban, which continues channelling these funds to its ideological allies, sustaining the vicious cycle of terrorism and crime.

Afghanistan is acting as a generous host for terrorist outfits that use its soil to generate economic rents through narco-trafficking, ransom, and smuggling. These groups are confident that their base of operation and sources of income would remain protected under the Taliban. A mere look at the figures showing the funding of this terror haven by the unsuspecting international community indicates why Taliban-ruled Afghanistan remains a threat.

Since August 2021, donors have confirmed paying $8 billion in humanitarian assistance to Afghanistan through various international organisations, with a further $3.78bn committed and $94 million pled­ged. Billions more were reportedly received by the Taliban regime in direct foreign grants. Western capitals remain the biggest financial contributors to Afghanistan.

The Afghan economy is kept afloat by foreign patronage and narco-trafficking.

Afghanistan imports goods worth around $12bn annually against exports of only $1.7bn, leaving an annual trade deficit of $10.3bn. It is intriguing how the Taliban regime is bridging $10.3bn every year. The answer lies in $17.5bn in foreign grants received since 2021, besides billions of dollars earned through narco-trafficking. It is clear evidence of an economy kept afloat by foreign patronage and narco-trafficking.

But the biggest question is: should such a regime that hosts terrorist groups be kept afloat through the largesse of international donors who might soon feel the heat from violent groups based in Afghanistan? If the international community thinks it can wean the Afghan Taliban away from terrorists, it needs to have a look at Pakistan’s 17 sincere attempts through ministers, the national security advisor, special representative, and joint coordination committee interactions, besides internationally mediated parleys in Doha, Istanbul, Riyadh and Urumqi.

The reason for Afghan obstinacy is hubris and ideological narcissism that are pampered through foreign indulgence, despite zero concessions by the Kandahar-based ruling clique on political inclusivity, women’s education and minority rights. Pakistan has tried bilateral channels and has exhausted relevant regional fora like the trilateral group (Pakistan, China and Afghanistan) where in 2023 the Afghan foreign minister agreed to stop the flow of terrorism from Afghan soil.

The Moscow Format , which evolved out of a quadrilateral forum which was abandoned by the US after the Ukraine War, kept pressing the Afghan regime to abide by international counterterrorism obligations till its seventh meeting. However, it failed to elicit a positive response from the Taliban delegation during its eighth iteration at Mos­cow on Oct 5.

The Taliban’s reluctance to even acknowledge the need to stop the ac­­tivities of terrorist groups from Afghan soil betrays a mindset that is sympathetic to these violent groups. This should be a clear message to countries like Russia that are giving space to the Taliban regime in pursuit of narrow security goals.

The fact is that all development and connectivity goals like TAPI, Casa-1000, and Trans-Afghan Railway, as well as mines and mineral exploration in Afghanistan, would remain a pipe dream till the time the Afghan Taliban stop sponsoring terrorism.

The proceeds from illegal mining go to support the Taliban’s military infrastructure. Moreover, since 2021, it has established thousands of madressahs against 269 modern schools, replaced 90,000 teachers with around 100,000 Taliban-aligned clerics, abolished 18 university disciplines, rewritten 200-plus subjects and banned 679 textbooks.

Until the Taliban regime gives up its support for terrorism and provides verifiable guarantees to Pakistan, meaningful normalisation, regional connectivity and lasting stability will not materialise.
