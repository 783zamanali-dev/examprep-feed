ANY doubts over India’s total hegemony over world cricket have now been removed. Its tactic of sidelining Pakistan is more blatant than ever.

The India-led International Cricket Council on Sunday announced its activity programme for the 2028-2035 cycle and conveniently left Pakistan without hosting rights for any event until 2031.

The ICC, chaired by former Board of Control for Cricket in India secretary Jay Shah — who was re-elected unopposed for another term on Monday — and run by his fellow countryman Sanoj Gupta as the chief executive officer, unveiled venues for major world tournaments over the next five years and split them between India, England, Australia and New Zealand.

Australia and New Zealand were confirmed as hosts of the next T20 World Cup in 2028. The 2030 T20 showpiece will be hosted by England, Ireland and Scotland. England will also host the World Test Championship finals in 2029 and 2031. India, meanwhile, have been awarded hosting rights for the 2029 Champions Trophy while they will co-host the 2031 ODI World Cup with Bangladesh.

These decisions come despite Pakistan successfully hosting the Champions Trophy last year, where India’s politicking saw matches of its team shifted to Dubai and the final also being hosted there. That move saw the Pakistan Cricket Board agree to a reciprocal deal with the BCCI where Pakistan’s matches in ICC events hosted by India for the next five years would also be played at neutral venues, and it is also probably the rationale behind making Bangladesh co-hosts for the 2031 ODI World Cup.

Political ties between India and Pakistan remain strained and the former has not shied away from bringing the heat onto the pitch on several occasions. Politics will likely spill over once again if Pakistan qualify for the 2029 Champions Trophy in India.

India has pulled out all the stops in its blatant sidelining of Pakistan and the burning question remains whether cricket will be able to rise from all of this.
