THE prevalence of rape in Pakistan means it can be a dangerous place for females. Yet, the issue is left out of political agendas. The Punjab government’s plan to introduce an anti-rape law, and establish fast-track courts to expedite the trials of violence against women and children cases with a definite timeframe for their disposal, is welcome. The problem, however, is more complex. On one end are patriarchal social structures that condone crimes of power, and on the other, is the absence of official and judicial resolve. Resultantly, the high numbers are a reality check for the government and its claims about preventive actions. As per the provincial police chief, police stations across Punjab logged 1.24m complaints related to females. The Women Virtual Police Station alone recorded nearly 125,000, while 71pc of sexual abuse offenders were known to their victims.

There is no dearth of laws. The state has long failed in carrying out its duty to enforce them, and foster a setting where women’s safety matters. The Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act, 2021 is in place for the speedy investigation and trial of rape and sexual violence cases involving women and children. The Punjab government can adopt its provisions as modern forensic and medico-legal departments, DNA testing, alongside a sensitised police force are important. Meanwhile, victim-blaming, and out-of-court settlements, often attained through coercion, feed rape impunity. The burden of shame is for the perpetrator to bear. For better conviction rates, attitudes must change. Women politicians must play a greater role in raising awareness in conservative areas to ensure offences are reported, penalties known, and laws implemented. Additionally, countrywide training for specialised probes to secure convictions cannot be delayed. Rape scars for life. A pro-survivor response alongside pre-emptive measures should be central to Pakistan’s criminal justice policy. When rape happens so frequently, justice is the sole safeguard for women’s respect.

Published in Dawn, November 19th, 2026

## Key facts

- ★ Punjab police stations registered 1.24 million complaints regarding females, according to the provincial police chief.
- ★ The Women Virtual Police Station in Punjab alone received approximately 125,000 complaints.
- ★ Police data indicated that 71% of sexual abuse perpetrators were individuals already known to their victims.
- ★ The Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act was passed in 2021 to ensure rapid trials and specialized inquiries into sexual violence.

- **impunity** — freedom from punishment or exemption from the consequences of a crime
