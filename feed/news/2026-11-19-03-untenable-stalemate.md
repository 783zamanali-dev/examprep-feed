> Why it matters: This is a critical study of regional geopolitics and the limitations of diplomacy in the face of shifting US foreign policy.



NEARLY nine months into a conflict of choice that has rattled economies from Europe to Asia, there seem to be few agreeable options for the US where ending the Iran war is concerned.

No one — perhaps apart from Israel and some hawks in Washington — wants an escalation, while coming to an agreement on Iran’s terms would result in considerable embarrassment for Donald Trump. However, Pakistan, which has played a crucial mediatory role in the conflict, and other regional states still have expectations that a negotiated settlement can be reached. As Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar told the ECO meeting on Monday, “we have not given up hope” for peace. The Iranian foreign minister was also in Islamabad for the event, and praised Pakistan’s efforts to broker peace.

But the most sincere efforts by Pakistan or any other mediator will not bear fruit unless there is clarity in Washington. President Trump, as has been his practice, has oscillated between saying the war is “effectively over”, and threatening fresh strikes if talks fall through. This cognitive dissonance must end.

Over the past few days, there have been some low-key military engagements between the US and Iran. The US had reportedly attacked facilities on the Iranian coast, while Tehran hit back by targeting American sites in Bahrain. These exchanges have the potential to get out of hand and spark a bigger confrontation. Which is why the quicker the matter is resolved, the better.

The collective voice from the states of this region is clearly against war, while the majority of the American people also appear uninterested in fresh clashes with Iran. Therefore, it is time the White House faced the bitter reality that its ill-conceived Iran campaign — fuelled by bad Israeli advice — has failed, and committed to resolving the imbroglio peacefully.

The option of attacking Iran again must be taken off the table by the US. In fact, there should be a positive response to the Iranian proposal currently on the table, specifically where lifting the blockade, ending sanctions, and allowing access to Tehran’s funds goes. This may seem like ‘capitulation’ to Mr Trump, but these matters should have been thought through before launching this disastrous war.

Tehran should also show flexibility, particularly where free and unhindered access through the Strait of Hormuz is concerned. Should Washington opt for the diplomatic off-ramp, it will find that regional states, particularly Pakistan, Qatar and others, will be happy to facilitate dialogue. However, indecision and a damaging status quo that is affecting regional peace and the global economy cannot be allowed to continue. It is time the Trump administration did the right thing.
