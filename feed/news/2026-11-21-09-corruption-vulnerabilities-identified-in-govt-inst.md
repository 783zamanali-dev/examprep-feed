> Why it matters: Understanding government anti-corruption frameworks and institutional reforms is crucial for questions regarding governance and public policy in Pakistan.



The federal government has identified weaknesses that could create opportunities for corruption in ten institutions through a comprehensive ‘National Risk Assessment of Corruption Vulnerabilities’ survey, Law Minister Azam Nazeer Tarar said on Friday.

In a press conference alongside Information Minister Ataullah Tarar and Minister of State for Finance Bilal Azhar Kayani, the law minister, who joined via video, maintained that the report was not a “charge sheet”.

Rather, it identified weaknesses that needed to be addressed through reforms to prevent corruption and promote “merit and transparency”, he said.

Speaking about the assessment, Tarar said the Ministry of Law and Justice had coordinated with various agencies and institutions to prepare the report, which examined vulnerabilities across federal institutions using different indicators.

“Let me also say that no other government has made efforts like this until today,” the minister claimed, adding that the survey would also be shared with international agencies.

The minister detailed that ten institutions had been examined in depth because of the nature of their operations, particularly those involving finance-related matters, which presented opportunities for corruption.

He also highlighted “positive” steps taken by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif during his governance. He cited power generation projects and reforms in the electricity and energy sectors, saying the government, in collaboration with relevant departments, had undertaken a detailed and lengthy exercise to address various issues in the sector.

“As a result of these reforms, the government is expected to save Rs3,500 billion in the electricity and energy sectors,” he said.

He also recalled the privatisation of Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) last year, which Tarar said was conducted through an open auction and competitive bidding process after previous attempts failed to materialise.

“You will see positive changes in that in the coming days,” he added.

The minister also referred to the government’s plans to right-size departments deemed unnecessary. As an example, Tarar highlighted the closure of Pakistan Public Works Department (PWD), which he said faced allegations of corruption and “wastage” of money.

“It was closed and a package finalised for its employees. Some legal amendments are pending in the Assembly,” he said, adding that the government saved billions of rupees through its closure. “Now this work will be executed through smaller institutions or provincial departments.

Shifting his attention back to the report, the law minister stated that the report had highlighted “checks and balances, misuse of authority” to stop financial leakages.

“This has been done for the guidance of the federal institutions. Consider it a precautionary step,” he added.

“Twenty-five institutions were selected, over 300 indicators were used and the role of regulatory authorities was taken into consideration as well, and the need for planning reforms was felt,” he said, reiterating that the report was not a “charge sheet”.

He said the institutions were selected based on financial linkages, including the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR), Pakistan Railways, Sui Northern, National Highway Authority, as well as the Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP) and discos.

In FBR’s role, Tarar held that the agency’s “good work” had increased the number of taxpayers from 3.7 million to 7m.

“Similarly, for Discos, there were complaints of electricity theft and now more than 2.1m smart meters have been installed,” the minister added.

He also noted the digitisation of toll collection on motorways as well as reforms around pensions and the digitisation of BISP via digital wallets.

The law minister said the objective of the report was to move towards “self-accountability” and ensure federal institutions operate on the basis of merit and transparency without any lapses.

He said the report had outlined a strategy for 400 reform measures, while 74 “reform blocks” had been presented. He added that the completion period for the steps outlined was between two and five years.

Concluding his address, the law minister reiterated: “Instead of waiting around for others to point out our weaknesses, we should identify them ourselves and present them in front of the people, as this is part of the prime minister’s vision of good governance.”

The information minister, speaking at the press conference, maintained that the report was an effort to set “improved targets by identifying weaknesses in the system”.

He added that the report was a continuation of the federal government’s efforts to ensure transparency, “as it had previously done in the closure of Pak-PWD, PASSCO and Utility Stores Corporation” .

In November last year, the International Monetary Fund (IMF), in its Governance and Corruption Diagnostic Assessment (GCDA), had highlighted persistent corruption challenges in Pakistan driven by systemic weaknesses across state institutions.

The lending agency had demanded immediate initiation of a 15-point reform agenda to improve transparency, fairness and integrity.

The report had estimated that Pakistan could boost economic growth by about 5 to 6.5 per cent over five years if it implemented a package of governance reforms beginning within the next three to six months.

The report revealed systemic governance weaknesses across state functions and noted that the country was exposed to corruption risk generated by weaknesses in budgeting and reporting of fiscal information, and management of public financial and non-financial resources, particularly in capital spending, public procurement and the management and oversight of state-owned enterprises.

It also identified an overly complex and opaque tax system administered by tax and customs authorities operating with insufficient capacity, management and oversight.

## Key facts

- ★ The survey identified corruption vulnerabilities in ten federal institutions.
- ★ The Ministry of Law and Justice coordinated the assessment process.
- ★ The government expects to save Rs3,500 billion through electricity and energy sector reforms.
- ★ The report is intended to be shared with international agencies to improve governance standards.

## Vocabulary

- **Vulnerabilities** — Weaknesses that expose a system to potential harm or risk.
- **Meritocracy** — A system where advancement is based on individual ability rather than wealth or social standing.
