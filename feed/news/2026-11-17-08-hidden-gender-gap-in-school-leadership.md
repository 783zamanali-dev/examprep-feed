> Why it matters: It addresses gender studies and governance issues related to institutional equality and educational reform.



There are significant disparities in women's representation in leadership positions within Pakistan's public school education system, reflecting pronounced gender inequalities across provinces. According to the Provincial Annual School Census 202324, the representation of female teachers in principal and vice-principal positions remains extremely low. In Punjab, for instance, less than 1% of female teachers have been promoted to these leadership positions. Similar gender disparities persist in Sindh, Balochistan and Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa. Despite the magnitude of this issue, gender-based inequalities in educational leadership remain largely under-researched and inadequately addressed in policy discourse. A major contributing factor is the lack of comprehensive gender-disaggregated data on professional training, recruitment, promotions, career progression, retention, workplace harassment and women's representation in leadership positions. This data gap limits the ability of policymakers to identify systemic barriers, formulate evidence-based interventions and allocate resources effectively to promote gender equality across the education system. A review of existing education policies also highlights significant institutional and policy gaps. There is an absence of systematic mechanisms to assess structural barriers, including gender-based discrimination and institutional biases, that constrain women's career advancement. Furthermore, limited awareness and understanding of recruitment and promotion procedures, inadequate workplace protection mechanisms against harassment, and the absence of reliable gender-disaggregated leadership data further undermine women's professional progression. Addressing these challenges requires a comprehensive review of existing policies and institutional practices, alongside robust data collection, transparent career advancement procedures, effective workplace protection mechanisms and targeted policy interventions. Women's presence in school leadership enhances instructional quality, builds community trust and increases girls' access to education. Women leaders are linked to safer school settings, stronger teacher support systems and higher learning results, especially for girls, according to data from UNICEF's global analysis 2022. Research from South Asia also demonstrates that gender-responsive leadership improves school administration and aids in removing sociocultural barriers that disproportionately impact female teachers and students. According to UNESCO's 2019 report on educational governance in Pakistan, leadership disparities are closely linked to systemic HR bottlenecks, ambiguous promotion pathways and gender-blind policy frameworks that ignore structural disadvantages faced by women. These findings suggest a positive relationship between women's participation in educational leadership and improved learning outcomes in basic education. Although the National Education Policy Development Framework 2024 acknowledges systemic injustices throughout the educational system, it does not specifically address gender disparities in educational leadership. The framework highlights governance shortcomings, teacher shortages and inadequate implementation mechanisms, but it does not examine how these problems disproportionately impact women. For instance, section 12 of the policy emphasises the need for better management structures and governance issues, but it ignores the gendered hurdles that keep women out of these positions. This omission highlights a significant policy gap. The framework does not incorporate gendered indicators into monitoring frameworks, does not acknowledge the significance of women in decision-making roles for attaining equity, and does not offer measures for developing gender-responsive HR systems. In order to ensure that governance improvements benefit men and women equally and promote better educational outcomes for all children, it is imperative that these discrepancies be addressed. Understanding how gender-based barriers at the policy, administrative and community levels influence school performance and educational equity requires a closer examination of women's leadership within Pakistan's institutional, social and cultural context.

## Key facts

- ★ In Punjab, less than 1% of female teachers are promoted to principal or vice-principal positions.
- ★ The Provincial Annual School Census 2023–24 highlights severe under-representation of women in leadership.
- ★ Lack of gender-disaggregated data restricts evidence-based policy making for female career progression.
- ★ Structural barriers include workplace harassment, institutional bias, and lack of awareness regarding promotion criteria.
