> Why it matters: This case involves constitutional rights, the independence of the judiciary, and the executive's adherence to Supreme Court directives, all of which are essential topics for Pakistan Affairs and Governance.



ISLAMABAD: Eight members of the Pakistan Bar Council (PBC) on Tuesday called upon the judiciary to ensure implementation of the Supreme Court’s (SC) order directing incarcerated ex-premier Imran Khan’s transfer to Shifa International Hospital for medical treatment.

On Aug 18 , a three-member SC bench had directed the government to shift Imran to SIH within the next two days as it heard several petitions concerning his health. However, the government took the PTI founder to the government-run Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences for a check-up and then transported him back to prison.

On Monday, in an open letter to the chief justices of the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC), the SC and the Islamabad High Court (IHC), Imran appealed for medical treatment for himself and Bushra Bibi. He also said that he would only be able to prove his innocence in the various cases against him “if I stay alive”.

In a statement issued on Tuesday, eight members of the PBC said: “We call upon the judiciary to take up the former prime minister’s plea immediately and to save his and his wife’s lives by ensuring that court orders are implemented, that proper medical treatment is provided and that due process and a fair trial are guaranteed as mandated by the Constitution.”

The statement was signed by Abid Shahid Zuberi, Muhammad Maqsood Buttar, Shafqat Mehmood Chauhan, Abdul Sattar Khan, Munir Ahmed Kakar, Salman Akram Raja, Salahuddin Ahmed and Qazi Muhammad Arshad.

The PBC members urged the government and its agencies to implement court orders and ensure that both Imran and his wife received proper medical treatment in accordance with the law and the Constitution. The statement warned that the government and its agencies would be solely responsible for “imperilling” the lives and liberty of the couple.

“We the undersigned reserve our right to initiate legal proceedings to protect the life, liberty, and constitutional and legal rights of Imran and Bushra Bibi,” the statement said.

The statement also condemned “in the strongest terms” the alleged unlawful and unconstitutional treatment meted out to Imran and his wife, adding that the situation was endangering their lives, liberty and dignity.

“[Imran’s] letter reveals that the former prime minister and his wife have been denied their constitutional and human rights through trumped-up and false cases and that they are incarcerated in violation of the law, the jail manual and the prison rules,” the statement asserted.

The statement further added that Imran and Bushra Bibi were convicted in false cases and their appeals against these convictions had not been heard.

“Even their bail applications have not been fixed for hearing,” it said.

The statement further highlighted that Imran had been kept in solitary confinement and denied due medical treatment and access to his legal team and family members, jeopardising his and his wife’s health.

The statement also alleged that his sisters had been detained arbitrarily and unlawfully, while the SC’s Aug 18 order had been openly flouted by the government and its agencies in violation of the law and the Constitution.

“Every person in Pakistan is guaranteed the right to life and liberty under Article 9, and the right to due process and a fair trial under Article 10-A of the Constitution,” the statement said.

“These rights have been denied to Imran, his wife, his family and his party members,” it said.

“As patriotic Pakistanis who believe in the rule of law, we are ashamed to read Imran’s plea for implementation of the SC’s orders and for the proper medical treatment that has been denied to him and his wife,” the statement said.

Moreover, the Democratic Alliance, a confederation of lawyers led by former PBC vice chairman Abid Saqi, expressed grave alarm over what it described as the deteriorating health and unlawful detention conditions of Imran and his family members.

In a statement, the alliance asked the judiciary to take immediate notice of the matter and enforce its orders concerning medical treatment and access to family members.

It also called on the government to end Imran’s solitary confinement, provide him with comprehensive medical treatment and release family members held without cause.

The alliance’s statement further urged the international community and human rights organisations to take urgent cognisance of the “grave” situation.

“The life of a former prime minister and his family hang in the balance. Justice delayed is justice denied. The Democratic Alliance demands that the rule of law and human dignity prevail before it is too late,” the statement said.

HRCP, Amnesty express concern

Separately, the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan also expressed “deep concern” at what it called the state’s treatment of political prisoners, urging authorities to uphold their fundamental rights, irrespective of political affiliation.

Referring to the allegations Imran made in his letter, the HRCP said they required urgent and independent examination.

“HRCP stresses that violations of political prisoners’ fundamental freedoms are not a matter of personalities or political affiliations; they constitute an assault on the rule of law,” the commission said.

“Every prisoner is entitled to humane treatment, adequate healthcare, access to family and lawyers, and a fair trial. All relevant court orders must be implemented and allegations of mistreatment independently investigated,” it added.

Meanwhile, Amnesty International said the “persistent denial of adequate health care, family visits, and regular meetings with legal counsel” to Imran and Bushra were a “violation of Pakistan’s international human rights obligations”.

Narrating complaints made by the former premier about the treatment allegedly meted out to him and his wife, Amnesty said, “In denying Imran Khan and Bushra Bibi their rights, the Pakistani authorities run afoul their international law commitments.

“Article 10 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, to which Pakistan is a state party, guarantees that any person denied of their liberty ‘shall be treated with humanity and with respect’,” it pointed out.

Amnesty urged the authorities to “immediately provide adequate healthcare to Imran Khan and Bushra Bibi and restore regular access to family and lawyers”.

Recalling the detentions of Imran’s sisters, Amnesty contended that his family “has also been targeted”. While two of his sisters were released last week, Aleema Khan remains under detention in Kot Lakhpat prison, it noted.

“Political persecution of Imran Khan’s family, particularly of his sisters for advocating for his rights, must stop. This includes the immediate release of Aleema Khan, and the authorities must ensure that no further charges are brought against family members for exercising their rights to freedom of expression and peaceful assembly,” Amnesty stated.

## Key facts

- ★ A three-member Supreme Court bench ordered Imran Khan to be moved to Shifa International Hospital on Aug 18.
- ★ The petition was signed by eight members of the Pakistan Bar Council.
- ★ The signatories include prominent legal figures such as Abid Shahid Zuberi and Salman Akram Raja.
- ★ The plea highlights the constitutional requirement for fair trial and due process for all incarcerated individuals.
