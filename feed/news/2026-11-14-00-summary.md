## Worth a skim

**Beyond diplomacy** (Dawn · Laraib Sajid)

Lawyer Laraib Sajid says Pakistan's diplomatic gains, including mediating between the US and Iran, must turn into real investment. The SIFC has a large project pipeline, but investors look at legal and administrative conditions. After the 18th Amendment, many sectors like minerals, tourism and agriculture need provincial approval, and the SIFC cannot direct provinces. She suggests using existing bodies such as the Interprovincial Coordination Committee and Provincial Investment Committees.

- ★ The SIFC presented a pipeline of 38 projects worth about $40 billion in potential investment to a Senate committee.
- ★ The SIFC was formed in 2023 through an amendment to the Board of Investment Ordinance, 2001.
- ★ Section 10E of the BOI Ordinance lets the SIFC direct federal bodies but does not expressly cover provincial governments.
- ★ Provincial Investment Committees are set up under Section 15 of the BOI Ordinance.
- ★ The 18th Amendment strengthened provincial autonomy by moving responsibilities from the centre to the provinces.

[Read it at Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2044213/beyond-diplomacy)

**Ambition without scale?** (Dawn · Ehsan Malik)

Ehsan Malik, former head of Unilever Pakistan, reviews the new Auto Policy 2026-31. He welcomes its aim of making the industry competitive but warns that cutting tariffs too fast could destroy the skills of local parts makers. He says too many models keep volumes low and costs high, and export targets need real buyers. He also questions the proposed Automotive Development Levy.

- ★ Pakistan announced an Auto Policy for 2026-31.
- ★ The policy envisages about $6 billion in cumulative automotive exports over five years.
- ★ The proposed Automotive Development Levy is expected to raise Rs232bn over five years.
- ★ WTO rules restrict subsidies that depend on export performance.
- ★ Auto-part industrial clusters exist around Port Qasim and Lahore-Sheikhupura.

PM Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir said Pakistan wants full value chains, not raw mineral exports. Barrick Gold said it would stay a long-term partner in Reko Diq. Many deals involve Turkish, British, American, German and Japanese partners, mostly for Chagai and Pishin in Balochistan.

- ★ Pakistan finalised 13 joint ventures, MoUs and agreements at the Pakistan Mineral Investment Forum 2026 in Islamabad.
- ★ Barrick Gold, which owns 50% of the Reko Diq project, said it intends to remain a long-term partner of Pakistan.
- ★ The FWO and Ivanhoe Mines signed an MoU for mineral exploration and mining in Chagai, Balochistan.
- ★ Turkiye's MTA International Company and PMDC signed a joint venture to explore antimony, copper, gold and other minerals in Pishin, Balochistan.
- ★ According to ICMA, Pakistan's mineral reserves are worth about $8 trillion over 600,000 square kilometres.

**Copper, gold deposits identified at eight sites in Balochistan’s Chagai district: mining company** (Dawn · Khaleeq Kiani)

National Resources Limited (NRL) says it has found copper and gold deposits at eight sites in Chagai, Balochistan, after about 25,000 metres of drilling. The finds lie in Darigwan, next to its earlier Tang Kaur discovery. The company is preparing its first formal resource estimate under international standards. It plans further studies from 2027 and a bankable feasibility study by the end of the decade.

- ★ NRL identified copper and gold deposits at eight sites in Chagai district after nearly 25,000 metres of drilling.
- ★ One drill hole showed a 295-metre stretch with 0.43pc copper and 0.13 grams of gold per tonne.
- ★ The discoveries cover 5.6 square kilometres in Darigwan, next to the Tang Kaur copper-gold site.
- ★ NRL is a joint venture of Fatima Fertilisers, Liberty Mills and Lucky Cement and holds five exploration licences in Balochistan.
- ★ NRL has committed $100 million to mineral exploration in Pakistan.
- ★ NRL has partnered with LUMS to build a predictive AI model for exploration.

**OECD report reveals migration to developed countries is dwindling** (Dawn · AFP)

An OECD report says migration to its 38 member countries fell for a second year in 2025. New permanent immigrants fell to 6.1 million from 6.2 million. Humanitarian migration dropped sharply, while labour migration rose. Portugal, Spain and Italy saw big rises, while the US, Canada and the UK saw falls.

- ★ OECD countries recorded 6.1 million new permanent immigrants in 2025, down 1.6pc from 6.2 million.
- ★ Legal humanitarian migration to OECD countries fell 26pc in 2025, while labour migration rose 13pc.
- ★ Portugal saw a 139pc rise in permanent migrants in 2025, to 313,600.
- ★ The US still led OECD countries with over 1.2 million permanent migrants despite a 14pc fall.
- ★ India was the leading country of origin for migration to the OECD in 2024, ahead of China and Colombia.
- ★ The OECD working-age population is set to fall by 8pc by 2060.
