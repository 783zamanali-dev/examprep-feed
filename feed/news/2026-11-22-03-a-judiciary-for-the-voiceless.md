> Why it matters: Valuable case study material for judicial activism, animal welfare jurisprudence, and environmental governance in Pakistan.



FROM Islamabad to Lahore, from Rawalpindi and Multan to Karachi, Pakistan’s courts have been writing the same story. It began with the Kaavan judgement in Islamabad. In Lahore, Punjab’s Animal Birth Control Policy took shape before the division bench of Justice Ayesha Malik and was enforced by Justice Shahid Karim in Eiraj Hassan vs Government of Punjab. Back in the capital, Justice Khadim Hussain Soomro recognised animal sentience in Nelofar vs Chief Commissioner ICT. Justice Malik Javed Iqbal Wains has since taken up the cause of donkeys and other draught animals, Justice Shahid Karim has protected endangered monkeys, and the honeybee is now before the courts. Judges in different cities, years apart, have reached the same conclusion. The law must protect those who cannot protect themselves.

Kaavan spent more than three decades at Islamabad’s Marghazar Zoo, much of it in chains and, after his companion Saheli died in 2012, alone. In 2020, in Islamabad Wildlife Management Board vs Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad, the Islamabad High Court under chief justice Athar Minallah ordered his release along with the other animals at the zoo, and held that animals are sentient beings whose interests the law must respect. Every case since has built on that ruling.

In Punjab, the fight was over stray dogs, which municipal bodies had long met with poison and bullets. The killing never ended rabies. The WHO, the FAO and the World Organisation for Animal Health have set 2030 as the target for ending human deaths from dog-mediated rabies, and they rely on mass vaccination of dogs to get there. Litigation before the division bench of Justice Ayesha Malik helped produce a policy of sterilisation and vaccination. Culling continued anyway, until Justice Shahid Karim ordered the government in Eiraj Hassan to implement the policy in letter and spirit.

In Nelofar, decided on April 28, the IHC was shown photographs of dead dogs piled in a vehicle attributed to the Capital Development Authority. Justice Khadim Hussain Soomro banned the poisoning, shooting and indiscriminate killing of stray dogs in the capital and ordered the ICT policy enforced. The authorities were told to carry out an area-wise survey of the dog population, keep records of every animal captured, sterilised, vaccinated or euthanised, and frame procedures for capture and sheltering. He held that an animal is a sentient being entitled to be spared unnecessary pain and suffering, and that euthanasia must remain a rare exception.

Justice Soomro also reminded the state that mercy towards animals is rooted in the Quran and Sunnah. The Quran says every creature on earth and every bird that flies on its wings forms a community like our own (6:38). Surah An-Nahl, named after the bee, describes how God inspired the bee to build its home and bring forth a drink that heals (16:68-69). The Prophet (PBUH) reportedly spoke of a woman punished for starving a cat and a man forgiven for giving water to a thirsty dog. Article 227 of the Constitution requires our laws to conform to these injunctions, so a court that protects animals is applying the Constitution as written.

The same reasoning now reaches working animals. The Pakistan Economic Survey counts close to six million donkeys, alongside mules and horses. They haul bricks from kilns and pull carts through traffic in summers that cross 45°C, often without water, shade or veterinary care. In Altamush Saeed vs Government of Punjab, Justice Malik Javed Iqbal Wains has begun to set out their rights. For the families who depend on them, a healthy animal is also a secure income.

The courts have looked beyond our borders too. CITES-listed rainforest monkeys from Brazil were brought into Pakistan illegally, and half of the original 26 have died here. When the government tried to move the survivors from a rescue centre in Karachi to the Lahore Zoo, Justice Shahid Karim stayed the transfer in June 2025. Justice Khalid Ishaq later made the order final. WWF Pakistan has since recommended a species-appropriate sanctuary for the survivors and a study of whether they can be returned to Brazil.

The newest case is about the bee. The FAO estimates that three in four of the world’s food crops depend at least partly on pollinators, and heavy pesticide use is wiping out colonies here. Losing them would hit our food supply directly, and it would ruin the beekeeping families, many of them in Multan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, whose income depends on healthy colonies. Protecting the bee means protecting the farmer and the food on our tables.

Taken together, these cases show that animal rights sit comfortably alongside human and environmental rights. The One Health approach, backed by the WHO, FAO, UNEP and the World Organisation for Animal Health, treats the health of people, animals and ecosystems as one. Its reasoning is simple: most new infectious diseases in humans come from animals, so the way we keep, trade and treat them is a public health question. A vaccinated dog protects a child from rabies. A healthy donkey keeps a family fed. Articles 9, 9A and 14 guarantee life, a healthy environment and dignity, and the courts have recognised that these guarantees mean little while animals around us suffer.

Public pressure mattered too. The singer Cher campaigned for Kaavan for years and co-founded Free the Wild to press his case. In November 2020 she came to Islamabad to see him off to a sanctuary in Cambodia, where he now lives among other elephants. She brought the world’s attention to him, and a Pakistani court set him free.

The judiciary has done its share, but orders on paper still meet resistance on the ground. In September, a contempt petition had to be filed in Nelofar and in the Eiraj Hassan case because the killing of dogs in Islamabad and Lahore had not stopped. Governments now need to fund the policies the courts have upheld, train the staff who carry them out, and act against officials who ignore court orders. Parliament should replace the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act of 1890. The rest falls to us, in how we treat the animals we live alongside. The Quran calls them communities like our own, and our courts have begun to treat them that way.

The writers were counsel and petitioner in several of the cases discussed.

Altamush Saeed is a lawyer who holds an LLM in animal law, environmental law and human rights law and teaches animal law and food law at Kinnaird College, Lahore.

Eiraj Hassan is a social media manager for the PSL at the Pakistan Cricket Board.

## Key facts

- ★ The Islamabad High Court recognized animal sentience in the 2020 Kaavan judgement (Islamabad Wildlife Management Board vs Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad).
- ★ Justice Khadim Hussain Soomro recognized animal sentience in Nelofar vs Chief Commissioner ICT.
- ★ Punjab adopted the Animal Birth Control Policy following judicial proceedings before Justice Ayesha Malik and Justice Shahid Karim.
- ★ The WHO, FAO, and World Organisation for Animal Health have set 2030 as the deadline to end human deaths from dog-mediated rabies through mass dog vaccination.

## Vocabulary

- **sentience** — the ability to feel, perceive, or experience subjectively
- **draught** — an animal used for pulling loads
- **culling** — killing selected animals to reduce group numbers
