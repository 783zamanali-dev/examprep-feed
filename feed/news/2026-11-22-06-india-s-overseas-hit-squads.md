The US has sentenced Indian national Nikhil Gupta to 19 years' imprisonment for plotting to kill a Sikh dissident, allegedly at the behest of the Indian government. Mention of the connection in the opinion, official statements and other legal documents establishes that US officials have no doubt that members of the Indian government have ties to the assassination, though blame has not been directly assigned to high-level officials or Indian intelligence. However, court documents and US Justice Department statements confirm that coconspirator Vikash Yadav - who the US believes is in India - was working at the Indian Cabinet Secretariat for the duration of the plot. This is significant because the Cabinet Secretariat oversees RAW, India's foreign intelligence service, which Pakistan and others have long accused of running international hit squads. The plot was for Yadav, through Gupta, to pay a hitman $100,000 to do the job. The only problem was that the operative Gupta hired was an undercover law enforcement officer. If not for the fact that the plot was infiltrated, things could have been much worse - Gupta had told the undercover agent that "they had many targets", referring to dissidents and other opponents elsewhere. That statement is also another solid bit of evidence of the violence India is willing to use overseas to meet certain policy goals. Apart from other killings in different countries, a European Parliament report has linked Indian agencies to targeted killings, cyber-attacks and harassment of critics living abroad, describing these as deliberate actions carried out under the direction of the Indian state. The report also says India has weaponised Interpol's Red Notices to illegally arrest activists, restrict their travel and cancel their passports, along with using spyware and social-media intimidation to further harass diaspora communities. The US and other affected countries must take punitive measures against India for the actions of its spies. Otherwise, it will be open season on dissidents, many of whom fled India specifically to escape New Delhi's repressive tactics.

## Key facts

- ★ The US sentenced Nikhil Gupta to 19 years of imprisonment for an assassination plot targeting a Sikh dissident.
- ★ Coconspirator Vikash Yadav was working at India's Cabinet Secretariat, which oversees the intelligence agency RAW.
- ★ The plot involved paying $100,000 to an operative who turned out to be an undercover US law officer.
- ★ A European Parliament report linked Indian state agencies to overseas killings, cyber-attacks, and the misuse of Interpol Red Notices.

## Vocabulary

- **dissident** — a person who publicly disagrees with and opposes government policies
- **weaponised** — adapted or exploited to attack or inflict damage on opponents
