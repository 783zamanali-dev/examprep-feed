## Worth a skim

**Ambition without scale?** (Dawn · Ehsan Malik)

Former Unilever Pakistan CEO Ehsan Malik questions whether the Auto Policy 2026-31 can meet all its goals at once. He welcomes tariff cuts and EV incentives but warns that cutting protection before lowering energy, tax and finance costs could destroy the engineering skills of local parts makers. He says localisation should measure real domestic value addition, and too many car models keep volumes small. He doubts the $6 billion export target and warns that a new levy may repeat the weak record of the Export Development Fund.

- ★ Pakistan's Auto Policy 2026-31 envisages about $6 billion in cumulative automotive exports over five years.
- ★ The proposed Automotive Development Levy is expected to raise Rs232bn over five years.
- ★ WTO rules restrict subsidies that depend on export performance.
- ★ Auto-parts industrial clusters exist around Port Qasim and Lahore-Sheikhupura.

[Read it at Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2044217/ambition-without-scale)

**Used car imports** (Dawn · Editorial)

Dawn says the government's curbs on used car imports are failing. Even after the personal baggage scheme was abolished, more than 2,000 vehicles came through the gift scheme in October alone. Imports of completely built-up vehicles rose 40pc to $126m in the first quarter of the fiscal year, adding to pressure on reserves and hurting tax collection. The paper warns this, together with the new auto policy, threatens the local parts industry and jobs.

- ★ More than 2,000 used vehicles reportedly arrived in Pakistan through the gift scheme in October 2026.
- ★ Imports of completely built-up vehicles rose 40pc to $126m in the first quarter of FY2026-27.
- ★ The personal baggage scheme for vehicle imports has been abolished.

[Read it at Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2044210/used-car-imports)

**Constrained partnership** (Dawn · Zafar Mirza)

Former health minister Zafar Mirza examines why the Global Fund placed Pakistan under its Additional Safeguard Policy in August 2020. A letter from the Fund cited rising HIV, weak TB results and poor performance of the Common Management Unit, whose head changed 10 times in three years. New HIV infections tripled between 2010 and 2024. He calls for a time-bound plan to exit the policy and a gradual shift to domestic funding.

- ★ Since 2003 the Global Fund has disbursed more than $1.1 billion to Pakistan's HIV, TB and malaria programmes.
- ★ The Global Fund invoked its Additional Safeguard Policy on Pakistan in August 2020.
- ★ The Additional Safeguard Policy has been invoked in about 30 countries but revoked only in Cuba, Myanmar and Côte d'Ivoire.
- ★ WHO and UNAIDS estimates show Pakistan's annual new HIV infections rose from about 16,000 in 2010 to 48,000 in 2024.
- ★ In 2024, 90pc of Pakistan's HIV financing came from the Global Fund.
- ★ The Global Fund, set up in 2002, has helped save an estimated 72 million lives.

[Read it at Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2044221/constrained-partnership)

**Midterm lessons** (Dawn · Asif Saad)

Asif Saad argues that the US midterm results show economic growth alone no longer wins votes. Despite record stock prices and low unemployment, US consumer sentiment stayed near record lows because of the cost of living. He questions trickle-down economics and says wealth for asset owners does not reach wage earners automatically. He warns Pakistan that stabilisation, privatisation and growth will not bring shared prosperity unless ordinary people feel the benefits.

- ★ US consumer sentiment remained close to record lows before the 2026 midterm elections despite stock market highs.
- ★ The writer identifies privatisation and deregulation as central to Pakistan's current reform programme.
