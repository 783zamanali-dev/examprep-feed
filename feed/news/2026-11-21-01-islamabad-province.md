> Why it matters: It addresses critical questions regarding federalism, administrative restructuring, and democratic governance in Pakistan.



OPPOSITION to it has been largely suppressed, but all is not well within the ruling regime. One way or the other, the proposal to break up existing federating units will ultimately shape the regime’s endgame.

Stage one has been completed without major resistance, the PTI disarmed through the imposition of emergency in KP , and a neo-administrative experiment comprising 12 districts in the southern and ex-Fata regions operationalised.

The ultimate prize for the establishment is the long-desired separation of Karachi from the rest of Sindh. And this is where the Bhutto-Zardari clan, facing an existential threat, will be forced to play its hand.

But before this, there are likely to be other moves expected to trigger less resistance, both within the ruling coalition and outside it. One of these is the plan to make Islamabad into a province.

The bureaucracy rules Islamabad without impediment.

The only proposal on record, finalised by a committee headed by Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal, has advised a 27-member assembly, headed by an elected chief minister/mayor. The elephant in the room is the viceregal institution which dictates the fates of Islamabad’s three million people — the Capital Development Authority (CDA).

Pakistani democracy is, at best, an aspiration rather than a functioning reality connected to the will of the people. Elected institutions operate under pressure, while most who claim to be representatives of the people wage class war daily. Nevertheless, the pretence of democracy remains.

The federal capital is a glaring exception. The bureaucracy rules here without impediment, the popular will be damned. There has been one citywide experiment with local government since the capital was created in 1960. The 2016 elections brought the Municipal Corporation of Islamabad (MCI) into existence, under the nominal authority of an elected mayor. The mayor and elected representatives down to the level of union councillor had virtually zero legislative and financial power. Elections never took place again, and the MCI was transformed into yet another bureaucratic feeder to the CDA.

So if Islamabad is given a legislative assembly, will the CDA be cut down to size? I choose my words carefully — because those who are poised to ‘give’ Islamabad an assembly have, over the past year or so, made their plans for the city and its environs sufficiently clear.

A short list of ‘achievements’ reads as follows: thousands of homes in katchi abadis and historical villages bulldozed; suburban sprawl in the form of land-grabbing real-estate schemes extended; thousands of trees razed and the city’s air poisoned by relentless construction.

With the CDA’s Master Plan flagrantly violated by its own commercial projects and big proprietors, another post-facto amendment in zoning regulations was recently approved by the government. As for what is to come, entire chunks of Rawal Lake and many villages around Bhara Kahu will be sacrificed for a 350-acre safari park, while the area adjacent to the Bari Imam shrine — where forced evictions took place earlier this year — is being given over to hotel developers.

Will Islamabad’s much-celebrated green spaces, already limited public services, and working-class settlements be left by the time the purported legislative assembly comes into place? Will the dominant imaginary of ‘development’ fronted by the state apparatus — of which the CDA is a poster-child — suddenly give way to a political-economic-environmental arrangement that serves the mass of Islamabad’s people?

Only the awfully naïve believe that the plans for an Isla­mabad province — which may ex­­tend into adjacent districts like Mu­­rree and Haripur currently part of Punjab and KP respectively — are driven by democ­r­atic impulses. Seen thus, the Islam-abad case provides the most obvious indication of what the new provinces hullabaloo is all about; an attempt to subjugate the political and economic interests of the mass of the population, and future generations to boot, all in the name of efficient administration.

Over the course of the past year, mainstream parties like the PPP and PML-N have made no effort to stand with katchi abadi dwellers and villagers whose homes have been bulldozed in Islamabad. They have not resisted the barrage of ‘development’ projects that have forever destroyed local ecologies.

Islamabad was always the preserve of the elite, but working people fought for their rights with some success, mainstream politicians paying heed in exchange for votes. Like in the rest of the country, Islamabad’s working masses now have to exclusively wage their own political fight. They are up against both powerful elements and political entrepreneurs who hunt with the hare and run with the hound.

The writer teaches at Quaid-i-Azam University, Islamabad.

Published in Dawn, November 20th, 2026

## Key facts

- ★ A committee led by Ahsan Iqbal proposed an assembly of 27 members for Islamabad.
- ★ The current federal capital administration is primarily governed by the Capital Development Authority.
- ★ Islamabad has hosted only one city-wide local government election since its establishment in 1960.
- ★ The proposed structure for the new province includes an elected chief minister or mayor.

## Vocabulary

- **Viceregal** — Relating to a viceroy or a system of absolute, appointed authority.
- **Impediment** — A hindrance or obstruction in doing something.
