Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik on Saturday declared climate change an “enemy” of civilisations as he highlighted its impact on South Asian countries at a meeting of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) in India.

Malik is in New Delhi on a three-day trip to attend the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum, making him the first Pakistani minister to visit the neighbour since former foreign minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari attended an SCO meeting in May 2023.

Addressing the forum on Saturday, the minister said the enemy of this civilisation was not another civilisation but climate change, noting that Nepal’s representative also spoke about the issue.

Malik recalled the Aug 26 floods in Nepal, which claimed over 1,400 lives and left thousands of others missing, as well as the devastating 2022 floods in Pakistan.

He noted that those monsoon floods left 1,700 people dead and submerged one-third of the country, including almost the entire Sindh.

“Four days of rain caused Mohenjo Daro’s walls to fall; those walls that no one was able to break for 4,500 years, climate [change] and rain-induced floods did it.”

The minister remarked that humans were also an “enemy”, as they contributed to climate destruction.

“Do you know what is the meaning of Mohenjo-Daro?” Malik asked. “It means mound of the dead.”

Malik said the ancient site had, in a sense, opened its gates to the living during the 2022 floods, providing refuge to people displaced by the disaster.

He said people had sought shelter wherever they could find it, including parking lots and shopping malls, while mosques, temples, shrines and schools in Balochistan had also opened their doors to those affected by the floods.

“This is the power of civilisation. This is the power through which nations survive,” the minister noted.

During his speech, Malik presented three proposals for SCO, including the establishment of a network of cultural institutions, a digital repository to present the region’s historical and natural “gifts” to the world, and a climate-resilient heritage framework to address destruction caused by climate change in the region.

At the outset of his address, Malik said he had initially intended to deliver his speech in English but felt more inclined to speak in Urdu after considering his proximity to the tombs of Urdu poet Mirza Ghalib and Sufi saint Nizamuddin.

“It felt a little strange to speak in English. So, with your permission, I would like to speak in Urdu,” he said.

The minister pointed out that distinct identities were needed for dialogue, which could only take place between diverse voices.

“If there is one identity, one voice, then a monologue is needed. There is no need for dialogue,” he said, comparing it to waking up in the morning, looking in the mirror and speaking only to oneself.

“There is no beauty in that. Beauty is [in] the vase that has colourful flowers, the smell of different kinds. Today, we’re here to celebrate that colourful vase,” the minister said.

During his address, the minister highlighted Pakistan’s history and how its civilisation had been shaped by multiple influences, with connections extending across China, Central Asia, Iran and the Middle East.

“When we look at our history, all our roads lead to the north; Bukhara and Samarkand and Central Asia and Afghanistan. The entire history takes us to the north,” he said. “And when we talk about our prayers, our poetry, we are drawn towards the Middle East and Iran or Persia.”

Malik said diversity and the free flow of ideas and identities were something to be embraced. “There is wisdom there, in embracing it. We’re here to celebrate these things today.”

Meetings with Indian minister, others

Dr Malik arrived at Purana Qila in New Delhi for the second day of the inaugural SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum, according to a post shared by his office earlier in the day.

On the sidelines of the forum, Malik held separate bilateral meetings with representatives from various countries, including host India, Nepal, Iran and Tajikistan.

He met with Indian Minister for Culture and Tourism Gajendra Singh Shekhawat and Indian Secretary for Culture Vivek Aggarwal, state-run APP reported.

According to APP , the discussions focused on cultural ties and opportunities for promoting cooperation between Pakistan and India, with both sides exchanging views on matters of mutual interest and the importance of strengthening cultural and civilisational links.

During his meeting with Nepal’s Culture and Tourism Minister Khadga Raj Poudel, the climate change minister expressed deep grief over the loss of precious human lives in the August floods.

Malik emphasised that urgent global action was essential to address the growing risks of natural disasters caused by climate change.

In his meeting with Tajikistan’s Minister of Culture Sattoriyon Amonzoda, Malik underscored the strong historical and cultural ties between the two countries, the Pakistan High Commission in India said.

He reaffirmed Pakistan’s resolve to further strengthen these multifaceted ties, particularly through greater people-to-people exchanges.

In a separate meeting with the president of Iran’s Islamic Culture and Relations Organisation, Mohammad Mahdi Imanipour, Dr Malik underscored the deep-rooted historical and cultural bonds between Pakistan and Iran.

He reaffirmed Pakistan’s firm commitment to further expanding these fraternal ties across all spheres, the high commission said.

Meanwhile, Malik and Azerbaijan’s Culture Minister Adil Karimli expressed their commitment to further strengthening the long-standing fraternal ties between Islamabad and Baku.

Malik emphasised the need to enhance cooperation across all sectors of the bilateral relationship between the two countries.

The climate change minister also met with Kyrgyz Republic’s Deputy Minister of Culture and Youth Policy Askaraly Shukuralievich.

A day earlier, after arriving in New Delhi, Malik had described the forum as a “tremendous opportunity” for multilateral dialogue, saying that cultures and civilisations transcended geographical boundaries.

Speaking to Indian news agency ANI , he had said the gathering offered an opportunity to discuss shared history, geography, cultures and civilisations.

Malik’s India trip comes two years after India’s External Affairs Minister Subrahmanyam Jaishankar visited Islamabad for an SCO meeting.

Pakistan currently holds the rotating chairmanship of the SCO Council of Heads of State for the 2026-27 term and is scheduled to host the organisation’s summit in Islamabad in August 2027.
