In October, U.S. President Donald Trump sold his eleventh-hour deal to secure diesel from Russian President Vladimir Putin—pausing sanctions that his own administration had imposed—as a boon for the negotiations to end the war in Ukraine. The arrangement, he wrote on Truth Social, was intended to keep Russia at “the bargaining table.” Of course, it was always obvious to observers that the gambit had as much or more to do with bolstering the U.S. economy and stemming a massive victory for the Democrats in this month’s midterm elections, and in that, it failed.Cynics have grounds for questioning all of Trump’s moves when it comes to Russia and Ukraine. He clearly dislikes Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and shows a marked preference for illiberal leaders, especially those willing to give him easy wins. Still, a stopped clock can be right twice a day. And concessions such as the fuel deal are exactly what might move Russia and Ukraine closer to peace, even if they are not wholly motivated by public-spiritedness.
