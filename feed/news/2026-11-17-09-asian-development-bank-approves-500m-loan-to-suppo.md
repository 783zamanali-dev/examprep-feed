The Asian Development Bank (ADB) on Tuesday approved a $500 million results-based loan to help Pakistan reform its federal civil pension system, the Manila-based lending agency said in a statement .

The reforms will help contain rising pension-related fiscal pressures, strengthen pension system sustainability, and expand fiscal space for development and social spending, according to the statement.

It added that Pakistan’s federal pension spending has grown nearly fivefold since fiscal year 2016 and now exceeds Rs1 trillion a year.

“Its longstanding unfunded and noncontributory legacy defined benefit scheme is financed entirely through annual budget allocations, creating growing fiscal pressure as pension obligations increase over time,” the statement highlighted.

“Pakistan has taken important steps to strengthen its federal pension system, and this program will help sustain and institutionalise those reforms,” ADB Country Director for Pakistan Emma Fan was quoted as saying.

“ADB’s support will help achieve lasting results by strengthening institutions, improving transparency, and supporting a more sustainable pension system,” Fan added.

Furthermore, the financing will support the first phase of the government’s Pension Reform Implementation Plan, and the transition to the defined contribution pension scheme, in which federal civil employees appointed from July 2024 are required to participate, said the statement.

“ADB’s support will help improve pension management to reduce liabilities, boost financial transparency through digital systems, and enhance pension service delivery and financial literacy among beneficiaries,” it read.

In 2024, ADB’s senior economist Aiko Kikkawa had said the pension system in Pakistan suffered from low contribution rates, making it unsustainable in the long run.

An earlier report by the ADB highlighted Pakistan’s weak pension and insurance systems. Most government positions entail unfunded pensions, where pensions for current employees are financed through tax revenues, according to the report titled ‘Strengthening Pakistan’s Pension and Insurance Systems’.

In August, the government executed agreements with 16 pension fund managers to formally operationalise the Defined Contribution Pension Fund Scheme (DCPFS), launched a year ago for public sector employees as part of major pension reforms aimed at containing the snowballing pension burden on the budget.

## Key facts

- ★ The loan amount is $500 million.
- ★ Federal pension spending now exceeds Rs1 trillion annually.
- ★ Pension costs have increased fivefold since the 2016 fiscal year.
- ★ The loan supports the transition to a defined contribution pension scheme.
- ★ Employees appointed from July 2024 are covered under the new pension rules.

## Vocabulary

- **Fiscal** — Related to government revenue, taxes, and debt.
- **Sustainability** — The ability to be maintained at a certain rate or level.
- **Defined contribution** — A type of retirement plan where the amount paid is fixed based on contributions rather than a set final benefit.
